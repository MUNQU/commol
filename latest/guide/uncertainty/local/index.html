    Local Approximations: Laplace, Sandwich and Importance Sampling¶

    Back to Uncertainty

    These methods give fast uncertainty around a fit from the curvature of the objective there, with a few runs per parameter instead of one fit per replicate. The Laplace approximation is a normal centred on the fit; the delta method gives the band of the outputs from it with no run per draw; the sandwich holds when the noise model is wrong or the noise is dependent, and needs no noise model; the Laplace mixture puts a Laplace approximation at each optimum of a multi-start fit and corrects it towards the posterior by importance sampling, with a check of when that fails. They run in Rust (crates/commol-inference/src/local/) through Calibrator.uncertainty.

    Quick path¶

    import numpy as np

from commol import (
    Aggregate,
    CalibrationParameter,
    CalibrationProblem,
    Calibrator,
    Laplace,
    LaplaceMixture,
    LikelihoodTarget,
    ModelBuilder,
    NelderMeadConfig,
    NormalObservations,
    ObservedDataPoint,
    Priors,
    Sandwich,
    Simulation,
)


def flow_model(rate: str):
    """A flow from A to B each step, of the rate `rate`."""
    return (
        ModelBuilder(name="Flow")
        .add_bin(id="A", name="A")
        .add_bin(id="B", name="B")
        .add_parameter(id="c", value=None)
        .add_transition(id="t_ab", source=["A"], target=["B"], rate=rate)
        .build(
            Aggregate(bin_amounts={"A": 1000, "B": 0}),
            engine="DifferenceEquations",
        )
    )


# The flow of each of 30 steps, 10 plus a normal noise of sd 1.
rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
flows = 10.0 + rng.normal(0.0, 1.0, 30)
problem = CalibrationProblem(
    observed_data=[
        ObservedDataPoint(at=step, output="B", value=float(value), window=1)
        for step, value in enumerate(flows, start=1)
    ],
    parameters=[
        CalibrationParameter(id="c", parameter_type="parameter", min_bound=0.0, max_bound=30.0)
    ],
    observation_models={"B": NormalObservations(sd=1.0)},
    optimization_config=NelderMeadConfig(max_iterations=500),
    seed=3,
)
calibrator = Calibrator(Simulation(flow_model("c * A / A")), problem)

laplace = calibrator.uncertainty(Laplace(draws=2000))
lower, upper = laplace.sample.interval(0.9)["c"]
print(round(lower, 2), round(upper, 2))  # 9.64 10.25

band = calibrator.delta_band(laplace, [10, 20, 30], 0.9)
print([round(value, 1) for value in band.center["B"]])  # [99.3, 198.7, 298.0]

sandwich = calibrator.uncertainty(Sandwich(block_length=1, draws=2000))
print(sandwich.blocks)  # 30
# The noise of these data is smaller than sd = 1: the sandwich reads it.
print(round(sandwich.covariance[0][0] / laplace.covariance[0][0], 2))  # 0.71

    The curvature and the unbounded scale¶

    Every method of this page reads the curvature H of the objective at the fit: −log L of the observation models, or the loss. curvature chooses it:

    curvature What it is Runs Use it when
    "gauss_newton" (default) The expected curvature: Jᵀ W J, with J the derivatives of the predictions and W the Fisher information of each observation, plus the information of each noise parameter 4p + 1 (finite differences) The model fits and the noise model is right or close
    "hessian" The observed curvature: central differences of the objective 4p² + 1 The residuals are large, the mean is wrong, or the fit sits on a constraint

    The Fisher information of each observation model (Noise::information in crates/commol-calibration/src/likelihood/density.rs):

    Model Mean μ Noise arguments
    NormalObservations 1/σ² sd: 2/σ²
    LogNormalObservations 1/(μ²σ²) sigma: 2/σ²; with center="mean" also 1 and −1/(μσ) with μ
    StudentTObservations (ν+1)/((ν+3)s²) df and scale, and their cross term
    PoissonObservations 1/μ
    NegativeBinomialObservations 1/(μ + φμ²) overdispersion: the variance of its score, summed over the counts
    BinomialObservations 1/(n p (1 − p)), p = μ/n

    The Hessian steps are δ^{1/4} · dθ/du for a parameter with a bound and δ^{1/4} · max(|θ|, 1) for one without, where δ is the accuracy of the engine (machine precision, or the rtol of the ODE engine): the step that balances the truncation error of the differences with the error of the runs, and keeps every run inside the bounds. Each entry of either curvature has an estimated error. An entry whose error is above 1 % of √(H_jj · H_kk) is listed in notes and logged as a warning: the error on the scale of a correlation, so an off-diagonal entry near 0 is judged against its row and column, not against itself. A note changes no result.

    Every distribution lives on an unbounded scale: the logit of a parameter between two bounds, log(θ − a) with a lower bound only, log(b − θ) with an upper bound only, the parameter itself with none. The curvature there is H_u = D · H · D with D = dθ/du at the fit, and covariance is on that scale. Every draw maps back inside the bounds, so a fit near a bound gives a skewed sample, not draws outside it. A fit on a bound of its parameter (within 1e-8 of the width of its bounds) raises a ValueError: its variance on the unbounded scale is infinite there, and a profile likelihood describes it (MUNQU/commol#132).

    Laplace approximation and the delta method¶

    Laplace(draws, starts=1) fits the problem (Calibrator.run(starts)) and returns a LaplaceResult: the fit, the covariance Σ = H⁻¹ of the normal on the unbounded scale, the draws mapped back (sample, equal weights) and the notes. It needs observation_models: a loss has no noise scale, so its curvature is no inverse variance, and Laplace with a loss raises a ValueError. To get a Laplace approximation, give a model with a noise parameter, such as NormalObservations(sd="sd") with sd calibrated, or use Sandwich, which needs none.

    Every local approximation sits at the best of its starts: the initial guess, then rows of a Latin hypercube over the bounds, as several starts of Calibrator.run. One start (the default) can end at a local minimum; more starts find the best one of the optima they reach. Sandwich takes starts the same way.

    With a noise parameter the fit estimates the noise by maximum likelihood: for NormalObservations(sd="sd"), σ̂² = SSE/n, not SSE/(n − p). The Laplace intervals are then narrower than the classical ones by √(n/(n − p)), which is negligible when n ≫ p.

    calibrator.delta_band(result, positions, confidence_level) gives the band of every output by the delta method: the prediction at the fit plus and minus the quantile of the normal times √(J Σ Jᵀ), with J the derivatives of the output on the unbounded scale. It runs the model a few times per parameter, not once per draw, and for a model linear in its parameters it equals the band of the draws (Predictive(calibrator, result.sample, positions).band(confidence_level)). It reads only the fields of the result, so a result read back from JSON works.

    widths = [round(high - low, 2) for low, high in zip(band.lower["B"], band.upper["B"])]
print(widths)  # [6.01, 12.01, 18.02]

    Sandwich¶

    Sandwich(block_length, draws, starts=1) gives the covariance H⁻¹ B H⁻¹, where B sums the outer products of the scores of blocks of observations: B = G/(G − 1) · Σ_b s_b s_bᵀ, with s_b the sum of the gradients of the terms of block b (the clustered estimator CR1, Cameron and Miller 2015). The blocks hold every series in a window of block_length positions, as the bootstraps do; "auto" chooses the length of Politis and White (2004) from the residuals of the fit. It returns a SandwichResult: a LaplaceResult with blocks (G), block_length and the estimates of "auto".

    What the sandwich protects against depends on H:

    curvature It holds when
    "gauss_newton" The noise model is wrong (its size changes, it is not normal, it is dependent inside a block) but the mean is right
    "hessian" Also when the mean is wrong: the fit estimates the parameters that minimize the expected objective

    It needs no noise model: with a loss, H and the scores are those of the loss (the sum of squares and the root mean squared error share them, since rescaling the objective does not change the sandwich).

    • A Student t with G − 1 degrees of freedom. With few blocks, B is a noisy estimate; the t widens the intervals by the right amount, instead of a warning at an arbitrary number of blocks. Intervals of the draws and Calibrator.delta_band both read it.
    • G ≤ p is refused. B sums G outer products whose sum has rank at most G − 1 after the fit, so with no more blocks than parameters some directions would get zero variance. The ValueError suggests shorter blocks.
    • loss_function="mae" is refused. The absolute error has no curvature: its sandwich needs a density of the residuals at 0 and a bandwidth, a choice the library would hide. Use a bootstrap.

    Laplace mixture and importance sampling¶

    A multi-start fit can end at several optima. LaplaceMixture(draws, target, starts=1) runs Calibrator.run(starts), keeps the distinct ends (an end closer than one Mahalanobis unit to a kept one merges into it), puts a Laplace approximation at each, weights each by its Laplace evidence, draws from the mixture, and weights every draw by the target over the mixture on the unbounded scale. The largest weights are smoothed by Pareto-smoothed importance sampling (PSIS, Vehtari et al. 2024).

    target Density
    LikelihoodTarget(priors) prior(θ) · L(θ): the posterior of the observation models
    GibbsTarget(priors, eta) prior(θ) · exp(−η · loss(θ)): the Gibbs posterior of the loss 
    # A flow c² has the two optima ±√ȳ.
square = problem.model_copy(
    update={
        "parameters": [
            CalibrationParameter(
                id="c", parameter_type="parameter", min_bound=-5.0, max_bound=5.0,
                initial_guess=1.0,
            )
        ],
        "observed_data": [
            ObservedDataPoint(at=step, output="B", value=float(value), window=1)
            for step, value in enumerate(4.0 + rng.normal(0.0, 1.0, 10), start=1)
        ],
    }
)
mixture = Calibrator(Simulation(flow_model("c * c * A / A")), square).uncertainty(
    LaplaceMixture(
        starts=8, draws=2000, target=LikelihoodTarget(priors=Priors.uniform_on_bounds())
    )
)
print([round(component.center["c"], 2) for component in mixture.components])  # [2.05, -2.05]
print(round(mixture.k_hat, 2), mixture.reliable)  # 0.24 True

    It returns an ImportanceResult: the fit, the weighted sample, the components (centre, weight, covariance, log evidence), k_hat, the effective_size and reliable.

    Reading k_hat¶

    k_hat is the estimated shape of the tail of the importance weights: how far the mixture misses the target.

    k_hat Meaning
    Up to 0.5 The weights have a finite variance: the estimates converge at the usual rate
    0.5 to 0.7 They converge, more slowly: use more draws
    Above 0.7 The mixture misses mass of the target, as for a curved (banana) posterior: reliable is false, notes and a warning say so, and the sample should not be used

    A start that ends on a bound gives no component, and notes says so. When its objective is at least as good as that of the worst kept component, the mixture leaves out an optimum the data support, whose mass k_hat cannot see: reliable is false too. An end on a bound that fits worse than every component only adds the note.

    An unreliable sample calls for a method that samples the posterior itself: BayesianPosterior or GibbsPosterior (Sequential Monte Carlo).

    When a local approximation is good enough¶

    Situation Good enough?
    A model near linear in its parameters around the fit, a correct noise model Laplace, with Calibrator.delta_band for the outputs
    A wrong or unknown noise model, dependent residuals, a right mean Sandwich with "gauss_newton" and blocks longer than the dependence
    A mean that may be wrong, large residuals Sandwich with "hessian"
    Several optima of equal or similar loss Laplace mixture; check k_hat
    A curved posterior, a parameter whose data barely bound it, a fit on a bound No: sample the posterior (Sequential Monte Carlo), or a bootstrap (Resampling)

    Checklist¶

    • [ ] The fit is a minimum inside the bounds; no ValueError names a parameter on its bound.
    • [ ] notes is empty, or you know why an entry of the curvature is uncertain.
    • [ ] A Laplace approximation has a noise model you trust; otherwise use the sandwich.
    • [ ] A sandwich has more blocks than parameters, of a length that holds the dependence.
    • [ ] A Laplace mixture has k_hat at most 0.7.
