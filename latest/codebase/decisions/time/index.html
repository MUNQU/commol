    Time¶

    Back to Design decisions

    How the axis works: its units, the time inside a step, impulses, windows and positions.

    Physical units are not in the model¶

    A Model records no step length: rates are per step and positions and periods are step counts. The axis of a model is its independent variable, whatever quantity it is (time is one case), so the engines never ask which dimension it has. Model.step_unit is optional; only the unit check and AxisUnits read it. AxisUnits (context/axis_units.py) converts a value in any unit of the step dimension to steps on pint, and Model.axis_units() builds it from step_unit (or step) and the engine of the model. steps returns a float and snaps a result within 1e-9 of an integer to it; whether a position must be whole is the engine's check, not the converter's.

    The axis owns the window grid: AxisUnits.window_start, window_end, window_index and window_ends take the width as a span and a unit, as steps does, and check that it is a positive whole number of steps. They call the grid in steps of context/_windows.py, private, the one grid that windowed_totals and _observed_report read; those two take a window of any positive length, because the window of an observation of a differential model need not be whole. No public function takes a width in steps: a width of n steps is the span n in the step unit. The grid is in the context layer, so AxisUnits does not import api/.

    The rate conversions (rate_from_mean_span, rate_from_probability) ask the engine: RateMeaning::rate_from_mean_span and rate_from_probability (crates/commol-core/src/types/engine.rs) hold the formula of each meaning, so a new engine picks a meaning and Python does not change. The per-step rate from a mean span is 1 - e^(-1/n): it keeps the survival of the continuous process at every step, and its mean residence is 1/rate, not n (10.508 steps for n = 10). r = 1/n keeps the mean and loses the survival.

    Rule: no formula of a rate per engine in Python; ask the engine.

    Guards: tests/simulation/test_axis_units.py, crates/commol-core/src/types/engine.rs (tests a_rate_from_…), tests/simulation/test_windows.py::TestWindowGrid, tests/simulation/test_axis_units.py::TestWindowGrid.

    An origin puts the axis on the calendar¶

    AxisUnits.origin (context/axis_units.py) is the calendar moment of position 0. It belongs to the axis, so ModelBuilder declares it next to step_unit, Model.origin keeps it and Model.axis_units() passes it on. On a Simulation, two runs of one model could read one position as two dates. The engines never see it: it converts at the edges, as the step unit does.

    The origin and every moment are naive (naive_moment): a date is its midnight, and a moment with a time zone raises ValueError with the way to convert it. A step has no time zone, so position = (moment - origin) / step stays exact; converting to UTC would move the end of a day with summer time, and dropping the zone would lose information.

    An origin needs a step unit of time whose length is a whole number of days or a whole fraction of a day (seconds_per_step). pint defines month and year with a fixed length (a month is 1/12 of a year), so with an origin on 1 January, moment_of(1) in months would be 31 January 10:30, not 1 February. The test of a whole number of days, or of days per step, rejects them and every unit made from them without a list of names.

    A day or a week ends at the midnight that starts the next one. day_ends and week_ends give these ends in (start, end], the interval of a window, from the first midnight after start; the first end can close a partial period. first_weekday numbers days as date.weekday does and has no default: which day starts a week is the user's choice. The number of a period is not on the axis.

    plot.series of results labels the ticks with _date_label (the date, and the time when it is not midnight) when the model has an origin and the call has no position_label. With no tick_every, it formats the ticks that matplotlib places, so a long run does not get one label per position.

    Rule: a conversion between dates and positions goes through AxisUnits; an application keeps no origin of its own.

    Guards: tests/units/test_calendar_axis.py, tests/plotting/test_run_figures.py::test_an_axis_with_origin_labels_ticks_with_dates.

    One step unit in the unit check¶

    The engine converts no value between units. So UnitChecker._step_unit (context/_model/units.py) uses one step unit: the declared Model.step_unit, of any single dimension (check_step_unit), or else step, the default increment of any axis (dimension [step]). The check never infers the step unit from the parameters: a parameter in 1/meter may be a density, not a rate along the axis, and inferring only from units of time gives time a privilege that no other axis has. Without step_unit, a failed check says to declare it. The check reads the flows the engine plans (transition_flows, in UnitChecker._collect_unit_errors), so a proportional rate is checked as its flow, rate × source, and never by a rule of its own in Python. check_equation_units requires every flow to be equal to <bin unit>/<step unit>, and every impulse to <bin unit>, not only convertible to it (utils/equations.py, same_unit).

    A unit means what pint defines; there is no list of aliases. A name that pint does not define becomes the one unit of its own dimension (define_units). In the check, t (a position) has the step unit and step (a count) is dimensionless. A number written in a rate and added to, subtracted from or compared with a quantity is read in its unit, as the engine reads it (parse_equation_unit); a computed number stays dimensionless.

    Rule: never accept a convertible unit as equal. 1/week and 1/day in one model would read a weekly rate as a daily one.

    Guards: tests/units/test_model_units.py::TestConsistentModels (test_a_proportional_rate_is_checked_as_its_flow), tests/units/test_time_units.py::TestStepUnit, tests/units/test_time_units.py::TestAxisUnit, tests/units/test_time_units.py::TestPositionOnTheAxis.

    Time inside a step¶

    A differential model integrates in continuous time, but on [n, n + 1) its rates see step = n: t is continuous, step is the floor of t. A rate that reads step or a "pulse" series (and so AxisPattern.pulse, pulses, periodic) is therefore constant on its unit interval. When the right-hand side reads one of them (CompiledModel::depends_on_step), the engine ends a piece of integration at every integer, so each jump of the rate is the start of a piece (breakpoints in crates/commol-differential/src/solve.rs).

    A "step_function" or "linear" series reads t (SeriesParameter::evaluate), so it holds or interpolates from any position. The engine ends a piece at each of its points (CompiledModel::series_points), where the series jumps or bends. A difference engine reads t = step, so a series gives its value at each step, and it rejects a point between integers.

    A rate can also jump where t reaches a value it compares t with (t >= t_on, and so AxisPattern.interval and linear_ramp). The compiler collects those values when they read only numbers, pi, e and parameters that do not change during a run (time_thresholds in crates/commol-model/src/builder.rs), and the engine evaluates them with the current parameters and ends a piece there. Without that, an adaptive solver whose rate is 0 before a short window grows its step and steps over the window. A comparison with the state is a state event, which the engine does not detect.

    On a piece [start, end) the right-hand side sees t no larger than the float just below end (RightHandSide::last_t). A Runge–Kutta step evaluates the right-hand side at the end of its interval; without the limit, a value that jumps at end would already act on the last step of the piece.

    Whether an expression reads step comes from reads_variable, not from get_variables: get_variables leaves out the special variables, because it lists the variables a model must define.

    Consequence: a proportional pulse p moves p of its source in a difference model and 1 − e^(−p) in a differential model. An impulse moves p in both. The alternative, step = t, makes a pulse step == n true on a set of zero length, so it would never act.

    Guards: tests/simulation/test_differential.py::TestTimeInsideAStep, crates/commol-differential/src/tests.rs::a_pulse_rate_is_a_constant_rate_on_its_unit_interval, a_rate_reads_the_step_of_its_unit_interval, a_rate_that_switches_on_t_acts_only_on_its_window, a_linear_series_matches_the_integral_of_its_interpolant, a_step_function_series_holds_from_a_position_between_steps, crates/commol-model/src/builder.rs::a_comparison_of_t_with_a_fixed_value_is_a_threshold, tests/axis_patterns/test_pattern_runs.py::TestDifferentialIntegration.

    Impulses come before the flows of their step¶

    At step n the state jumps first, then the flows of the step run from the new state, in every engine of the aggregate kind (CompiledModel::apply_impulses; Units::apply_impulses for the stochastic engines). Every jump of one step is computed from the state before any of them. The output at n shows the state after the jump. With the other order, the differential output at n would show the state before the jump and the difference output the state after it, so the two engines would disagree at the output steps. A difference model needs integer impulse steps; a differential model stops its integration at each impulse step and restarts from the new state, so diffsol needs no event.

    Guards: tests/transitions/test_impulses.py::TestJump, crates/commol-differential/src/tests.rs::an_impulse_moves_its_fraction_at_once_in_both_engines.

    Windowed observations¶

    An observation at at with window = W is compared with output(at) - output(at - W), anchored at its own position. The Rust loss and windowed_totals (api/windows.py) must give the same number, and the predictive reads windows through the observation operator (One observation operator). Window w covers (w · W, (w + 1) · W]; window_index places a position with max(ceil(at / W) − 1, 0), and the observed block of a report (_observed_report in api/reporting.py) calls it. plot.series of results reads the model of a windowed observation the same way, through its own window at its own position, never on a grid of windows from 0: an observation at 10.5 with a window of 7 falls on no window end of that grid.

    Guards: tests/simulation/test_windows.py::TestAgreementWithTheCalibrationLoss (the loss at the generating parameters is zero), tests/reporting/test_simulation_report.py::test_observed_report_places_each_observation_by_window_index tests/plotting/test_band_figures.py::TestPredictiveSeries::test_a_windowed_band_of_one_draw_is_its_windowed_run and tests/plotting/test_figures.py::test_each_observed_point_is_read_through_its_own_window_and_position.

    The engine owns positions¶

    A position on the axis is an f64 in every engine call and a float in every public Python name (at); a length is window or period; a count of steps keeps num_steps. The SimulationEngine trait (crates/commol-core/src/simulation_engine.rs) runs only at positions (run_at_positions_into_buffer); run(n) runs on the grid the engine builds, grid(n), and returns it. Each engine says which positions its model type accepts (check_position): the difference engine and the chain binomial (StochasticSteps) non-negative integers, the differential engine and the events engine (StochasticEvents) any finite, non-negative position. The engine checks every position where it receives it, and its message names the object that holds a rejected one: the observation or constraint (check_positions in crates/commol-calibration/src/calibration_problem.rs), the impulse transition or series parameter when the model is built or its parameters change (check_model in crates/commol-model/src/builder.rs, for an engine that steps only at integers), or the positions of a predictive before any draw runs (Predictive::new in crates/commol-inference/src/predictive.rs).

    Rule: Python passes positions on and shows the engine's message. It never checks whether a model type accepts a position.

    Guards: crates/commol-calibration/tests/test_positions.rs, crates/commol-difference/src/simulation.rs::integer_positions_are_accepted_and_others_rejected, crates/commol-differential/src/tests.rs::a_difference_engine_names_a_series_point_between_steps, crates/commol-model/tests/schedules.rs::an_engine_that_steps_at_integers_rejects_a_position_between_steps_at_the_check, tests/building/test_parameters.py::test_a_point_the_engine_cannot_run_is_rejected_at_build_and_update, tests/calibration/test_observations.py::TestPositionsAndEngines, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_predictive.rs::positions_the_engine_cannot_run_are_rejected_before_any_draw.

    Results carry their positions¶

    Simulation.run and run_at return SimulationResults (api/results.py): a dict from output name to values, with the positions in positions and the rows in rows(). A dict keeps results["A"], json.dumps and pandas.DataFrame(results) working; a reserved key could clash with an output name. Windows, reports and plots read a value at a position (index_at, which matches within a relative 1e-9), never at a list index, so a model type that reports between integers fits them. A predictive holds the positions it was asked for (Predictive.positions).

    Guards: tests/simulation/test_runs.py::TestResults, tests/reporting/test_simulation_report.py::TestNonIntegerPositions, tests/plotting/test_figures.py::TestDifferentialModel.
