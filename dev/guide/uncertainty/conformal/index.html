    Conformal Prediction Intervals¶

    Back to Uncertainty

    Conformal prediction gives intervals for observations, with a coverage guarantee that needs no noise model, no prior and no correct model. It answers "will the next observations fall inside?", not "which parameters fit?". Each variant scores how far observations the fit did not use fall from their predictions, and a quantile of those scores sets the width of every interval. Use it when you must state how often new data fall inside an interval, and you cannot or will not say how the data scatter around the model. Every variant runs through Calibrator.uncertainty, in Rust (crates/commol-inference/src/conformal/).

    Quick path¶

    import numpy as np

from commol import (
    Aggregate,
    CalibrationParameter,
    CalibrationProblem,
    Calibrator,
    ModelBuilder,
    NelderMeadConfig,
    ObservedDataPoint,
    Simulation,
)
from commol.conformal import SplitConformal


def ab_model(k: float | None):
    return (
        ModelBuilder(name="AB")
        .add_bin(id="A", name="A")
        .add_bin(id="B", name="B")
        .add_parameter(id="k", value=k)
        .add_transition(id="t_ab", source=["A"], target=["B"], rate="k")
        .build(
            Aggregate(bin_amounts={"A": 1000, "B": 0}),
            engine="DifferenceEquations",
        )
    )


# Noisy data of B at steps 1 to 80: fit steps 1 to 40, hold out 41 to 80.
truth = Simulation(ab_model(0.05)).run(num_steps=90)
rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
observations = [
    ObservedDataPoint(at=step, output="B", value=truth["B"][step] + rng.normal(0.0, 5.0))
    for step in range(1, 81)
]
fitted = [point for point in observations if point.at <= 40]
held_out = [point for point in observations if point.at > 40]

problem = CalibrationProblem(
    observed_data=fitted,
    parameters=[
        CalibrationParameter(id="k", parameter_type="parameter", min_bound=0.0, max_bound=0.5)
    ],
    loss_function="sse",
    optimization_config=NelderMeadConfig(max_iterations=500),
    seed=42,
)
calibrator = Calibrator(Simulation(ab_model(None)), problem)

result = calibrator.uncertainty(
    SplitConformal(
        calibration_data=held_out,
        score="absolute",
        by="series",
        confidence_level=0.9,
        positions=[85.0, 90.0],
    )
)
intervals = result.intervals.series["B"]
print([round(value, 1) for value in intervals.lower])  # [981.0, 983.9]
print([round(value, 1) for value in intervals.upper])  # [993.4, 996.3]
print(result.guarantee)

    result.intervals holds the intervals of each series at positions, result.scores the scores they came from, and result.guarantee what the coverage claim assumes.

    What the guarantee means¶

    A conformal interval of level 1 − α covers a new observation with probability at least 1 − α, averaged over the data and the new observation.

    It says It does not say
    Of many new observations, at least a share 1 − α falls inside its interval That one interval holds its observation with probability 1 − α given the data
    The claim holds whatever the noise, and even when the model is wrong That the interval contains the model curve or the true parameters
    A wrong model gives wide intervals, not wrong ones That the intervals are narrow

    The claim holds when the scores are exchangeable: any order of the scores of the held-out and the new observations is as likely as any other. Noise that is independent and of one distribution along the series is exchangeable; a trend in the errors, a change of regime, or errors that depend on each other are not. Each variant names its assumption in result.guarantee.

    The quantile of n scores at level 1 − α is the ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉-th smallest (conformal_quantile in crates/commol-inference/src/conformal/quantile.rs). With fewer than 1/α − 1 scores that rank is above n, so the interval is unbounded: -inf to inf. result.intervals.unbounded names the positions of each series where that happens. At level 0.9 a quantile needs 9 scores.

    Scores and pooling¶

    Every variant takes a score, a by and a confidence_level, none with a default: they change the width of the intervals, which is a modelling choice.

    score Score of y against ŷ Interval Use it when
    "absolute" |y − ŷ| One width per quantile The noise has one size along the series
    "normalized" |y − ŷ| / s, with s the root mean square of the observed values of the series in the problem Width proportional to s Series of different size share one quantile (by="all")
    "spread" |y − ŷ| / σ̂, with σ̂ the spread of the predictions at that point Width proportional to σ̂ The uncertainty of the prediction changes along the series, as here, where the sample's runs come together as B saturates

    by="series" gives each series (observation label) its own quantile; by="all" gives every series one quantile. Per series is the safer first choice: a small series does not borrow the errors of a large one, but it needs 1/α − 1 scores of its own.

    • score="normalized" with by="series" raises a ValueError: with one constant factor per series, its intervals are those of "absolute".
    • score="spread" needs predictions with a spread. SplitConformal and AdaptiveConformal take it from a sample, whose predictive gives the prediction (its weighted median) and the spread (its weighted standard deviation); without one they raise a ValueError. JackknifePlus and EnbPI take it from their own refits. RollingConformal fits once per origin, so it rejects "spread".
    from commol.uncertainty import ParameterSample, SampleMethod

sample = ParameterSample.equally_weighted(
    names=["k"],
    bounds=[(0.0, 0.5)],
    values=[[0.048], [0.05], [0.052]],
    method=SampleMethod(name="by hand"),
)
spread = calibrator.uncertainty(
    SplitConformal(
        calibration_data=held_out,
        score="spread",
        by="series",
        confidence_level=0.9,
        positions=[85.0, 90.0],
        sample=sample,
    )
)
widths = [
    round(upper - lower, 1)
    for lower, upper in zip(spread.intervals.series["B"].lower, spread.intervals.series["B"].upper)
]
print(widths)  # [6.3, 5.2]

    Which variant to use¶

    Variant Held-out data Fits Guarantee Use it for
    SplitConformal calibration_data One At least 1 − α, exchangeable scores Many observations, some of which you can leave out of the fit
    RollingConformal None: each origin holds out what follows it One per origin At least 1 − α per horizon, scores of a horizon exchangeable across origins Forecasts: intervals ahead of the data that widen with the horizon
    JackknifePlus None One per block At least 1 − 2α with one observation per block; the CV+ bound with longer blocks; exchangeable blocks Short series, where no data can be spared
    EnbPI None replicates Near 1 − α, approximately, under stationary and strongly mixing errors Time series with dependent errors
    AdaptiveConformal The stream of new observations One The long-run share of misses tends to α, with no assumption Data that arrive one by one and can change regime

    How to hold data out¶

    Variant What to hold out
    SplitConformal Pass the problem the observations to fit, and calibration_data the rest. For a forecast, hold out the latest positions, as in the quick path, so the scores measure errors ahead of the fit. The held-out points must not be in the problem.
    RollingConformal Nothing: give origins. Each fits the observations at or before it and scores the later ones, so the problem holds every observation.
    JackknifePlus, EnbPI Nothing: they leave blocks out of their own refits. A block holds every observation of every series at block_length consecutive positions, so dependence stays inside a block.
    AdaptiveConformal The observations that arrive after the fit, in stream.

    Split conformal¶

    SplitConformal(calibration_data, score, by, confidence_level, positions, sample=None, starts=1) fits the problem's observations (Calibrator.run(starts)), or reads a sample, scores calibration_data and gives intervals at positions for each series of calibration_data. A series of calibration_data is read as its observations define it: its outputs, factor, scale and window.

    Rolling origins¶

    RollingConformal(origins, score, by, confidence_level, positions, starts=1) gives intervals ahead of the data, with one quantile per horizon. Horizon k is the k-th observation of a series after an origin: a count, not a distance on the axis, because positions can be irregular. Each origin fits the observations at or before it (one refit per origin, from the fit of every observation) and scores the later ones. The intervals are around the fit of every observation, at positions, which must all be after the data; the k-th position is horizon k, so give positions spaced as the observations. A horizon with fewer than 1/α − 1 origins that reach it has an unbounded interval.

    from commol.conformal import RollingConformal

every_point = CalibrationProblem(
    observed_data=observations,
    parameters=problem.parameters,
    loss_function="sse",
    optimization_config=NelderMeadConfig(max_iterations=500),
    seed=42,
)
calibrator_all = Calibrator(Simulation(ab_model(None)), every_point)
rolling = calibrator_all.uncertainty(
    RollingConformal(
        origins=[float(step) for step in range(30, 71)],
        score="absolute",
        by="series",
        confidence_level=0.8,
        positions=[81.0, 82.0, 83.0],
    )
)
print(rolling.intervals.unbounded)  # {}

    Jackknife+¶

    JackknifePlus(block_length, score, by, confidence_level, positions, starts=1) refits once per block left out (Barber et al. 2021). Observation i of block b has the residual Rᵢ of the fit μ̂₋ᵦ that left its block out, and the interval at a point x is

    [ q⁻_α { μ̂₋ᵦ₍ᵢ₎(x) − Rᵢ } ,  q⁺_{1−α} { μ̂₋ᵦ₍ᵢ₎(x) + Rᵢ } ]

    where q⁺ is the ⌈(1 − α)(n + 1)⌉-th smallest value and q⁻ the ⌊α(n + 1)⌋-th smallest. The guarantee holds with no held-out set, under exchangeable blocks. With n scored observations in K blocks it is the bound of K-fold CV+ (Theorem 4):

    1 − 2α − min{ 2(1 − 1/K) / (n/K + 1),  (1 − K/n) / (K + 1) }

    With one observation per block, K = n and the bound is 1 − 2α. With longer blocks, jackknife+ is CV+ with the blocks as folds, so CV+ has no class of its own. The theorem assumes blocks of n/K observations each. cv_plus_bound in crates/commol-inference/src/conformal/jackknife_plus.rs computes it, and result.guarantee states it.

    from commol.conformal import JackknifePlus

jackknife = calibrator.uncertainty(
    JackknifePlus(block_length=4, score="absolute", by="series", confidence_level=0.9, positions=[45.0])
)
print(jackknife.guarantee)  # ... at least 0.7318 ... with K = 10 blocks of 4 positions ...

    EnbPI¶

    EnbPI(replicates, block_length, score, by, confidence_level, positions, starts=1) draws its own replicates (Xu and Xie 2021): each draws as many blocks as there are, with replacement, and fits with each observation's weight multiplied by the count of its block, so the blocks it did not draw get weight 0. The leave-one-out prediction of an observation is the mean of the replicates that left its block out; the point prediction at a position is the mean of the leave-one-out predictions there. No fit beyond the replicates is needed. A block left out by no replicate raises a ValueError that asks for more replicates: a block is left out by a replicate with probability about 1/e ≈ 0.37.

    Adaptive conformal inference¶

    AdaptiveConformal(confidence_level, step, score, by, stream, sample=None, starts=1) walks the observations of stream in order of position (Gibbs and Candès 2021). The interval of each point is the quantile, at the working level 1 − α_t, of the scores of the earlier points of the stream; then the point is scored and the working level moves:

    α_{t+1} = α_t + γ (α − err_t),   err_t = 1 for a miss, 0 for a cover

    So a miss widens the next interval and a cover narrows it. The long-run share of misses tends to α whatever the data. by="series" keeps one working level per series, by="all" one for all. The first points have few earlier scores, so their intervals are unbounded. result.intervals holds the interval given at each point before it was seen.

    from commol.conformal import AdaptiveConformal

adaptive = calibrator.uncertainty(
    AdaptiveConformal(confidence_level=0.9, step=0.05, score="absolute", by="series", stream=held_out)
)
series = adaptive.intervals.series["B"]
misses = [
    not lower <= point.value <= upper
    for lower, upper, point in zip(series.lower, series.upper, held_out)
]
print(len(adaptive.intervals.unbounded["B"]))  # 7
print(round(sum(misses) / len(misses), 3))  # 0.125

    Results, plots and reports¶

    Every variant returns a ConformalResult(intervals, scores, guarantee, failed, not_converged, block_length, block_length_estimates). A variant that refits lists each refit that failed in failed, named by its unit: "Origin <index> failed: <reason>" for RollingConformal, "Block <index> failed: <reason>" for JackknifePlus, and "Replicate <index> failed: <reason>" for EnbPI; each is also logged as a warning, and gives no score. not_converged counts the refits kept although their optimizer did not converge. Split and adaptive conformal do not refit, so their failed is empty and their not_converged is 0. JackknifePlus and EnbPI give the block length they ran with in block_length, and with block_length="auto" the estimate of each series, by label, in block_length_estimates, as the bootstraps give them; the other variants have no blocks, so both are None. intervals is a PredictionIntervals: its confidence_level, a SeriesIntervals per series (positions, prediction, lower, upper), and unbounded. plot.series(prediction_intervals=...) of a run, of replicates or of a predictive draws the band of each series on its panel. SimulationResults.report, SimulationReplicates.report and Predictive.report take prediction_intervals=... too, and add the intervals under the key prediction_intervals.

    run = Simulation(ab_model(0.05)).run(num_steps=90)
figure = run.plot.series(
    observed_data=observations, prediction_intervals=result.intervals, series=["B"]
)

    Checklist¶

    • [ ] The held-out observations are not in the problem, and come from where the new observations will.
    • [ ] Each quantile has at least 1/α − 1 scores; result.intervals.unbounded is empty.
    • [ ] The assumption in result.guarantee fits your data: exchangeable scores, mixing errors, or none.
    • [ ] For a forecast, you use RollingConformal, EnbPI or AdaptiveConformal, not one split of errors one step ahead.
