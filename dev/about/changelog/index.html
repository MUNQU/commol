    All notable changes to Commol will be documented in this file.

    The format is based on Keep a Changelog, and this project adheres to Semantic Versioning.

    [Unreleased]¶

    Added¶

    • Local approximations through Calibrator.uncertainty, on the unbounded scale of the parameters, with a fit on a bound refused: Laplace(draws), the normal N(θ̂, H⁻¹) of the observation models, and Calibrator.delta_band(result, positions, level), the band of the outputs by the delta method; Sandwich(block_length, draws), the covariance H⁻¹ B H⁻¹ with the scores summed over blocks (CR1) and a Student t with G − 1 degrees of freedom, which needs no noise model and refuses loss_function="mae" and too few blocks; LaplaceMixture(starts, draws, target), a Laplace approximation at each optimum of a multi-start fit as the proposal of Pareto-smoothed importance sampling of a LikelihoodTarget(priors) or GibbsTarget(priors, eta), which reports k_hat and marks a sample with k_hat > 0.7 as unreliable. curvature is "gauss_newton" (the expected Fisher information of each observation model, by default) or "hessian". See Local Approximations.

    • GibbsPosterior: the Gibbs posterior prior(θ) · exp(−η · loss(θ)) of the problem's loss, sampled by sequential Monte Carlo from the priors to the posterior; it returns a GibbsResult with the η it ran with. eta is required: a number above 0, InformationMatchingEta(block_length) (the curvature of the sum of squares or of observation models against the variance of the scores of its terms summed over blocks, with a deterministic engine) or BootstrapCoverageEta(replicates, block_length, confidence_level) (any loss), joined in the union Eta. See Sequential Monte Carlo.

    • ABCSMC and ABCRejection: approximate Bayesian computation on a required distance, LossDistance() or SeriesDistance(metric). With a stochastic engine each distance reads one fresh replicate of its particle, so the problem's replicates and replicate_summary do not apply, and the result says so. See ABC.
    • SMCResult: the sample, the level of each step, the log evidence, the value each particle keeps and the diagnostics of each step (SMCDiagnostics: effective size, resampling, acceptance rate and move count).
    • Priors: Uniform(), LogUniform(), Beta(a, b), Normal(mean, sd), LogNormal(mu, sigma) and Gamma(shape, rate), each truncated to the bounds of its parameter, and Priors (a mapping of parameter to distribution, or Priors.uniform_on_bounds()). Every sampling method takes priors with no default, and a prior whose bounds do not fit raises a ValueError that names the distributions that fit them. Their draws are the same on every platform. See Priors.
    • SampleIdentifiability(reference="prior", priors=...): the contraction of each parameter against its prior. See Identifiability.
    • Conformal prediction intervals through Calibrator.uncertainty: intervals for observations with a coverage guarantee that needs no noise model, no prior and no correct model. SplitConformal scores held-out observations; RollingConformal gives intervals ahead of the data, one quantile per horizon (the k-th observation after an origin); JackknifePlus refits once per block left out, with the CV+ bound for blocks of more than one observation; EnbPI draws its own replicates of blocks for series with dependent errors; AdaptiveConformal walks a stream of new observations with a working level that moves with each miss. Each takes a required score ("absolute", "normalized" or "spread"), by ("series" or "all") and level, and returns a ConformalResult (PredictionIntervals, the scores and the guarantee). See Conformal Prediction Intervals.
    • SimulationPlotter.plot_series(prediction_intervals=...) draws the band of conformal intervals, and simulation_report(..., prediction_intervals=...) reports them.
    • Observation models: CalibrationProblem(observation_models={series: model}) takes one model per series label, NormalObservations(sd), LogNormalObservations(sigma, center) (center="mean" or "median", no default), StudentTObservations(df, scale), PoissonObservations(), NegativeBinomialObservations(overdispersion) (variance μ + φμ²) or BinomialObservations(trials) (or the new ObservedDataPoint.trials of each observation), and the problem fits by maximum likelihood. A noise argument is a number or a calibration parameter of the new type "noise", which never reaches the engine. Observed values outside a model's support raise a ValueError that names the observation. A stochastic engine and normalize_observations need a loss. See Observation Models.
    • BayesianPosterior: the posterior of the observation models and its log evidence, on the sequential Monte Carlo sampler. GibbsPosterior on observation models is the fractional posterior prior · L^η.
    • DistributionNoise(): prediction intervals that draw from the observation models; commol.scores.log_score, the log density of held-out observations under the predictive, which needs DistributionNoise().
    • ParametricBootstrap(replicates): fits to data sets drawn from the observation models at the fit. ResamplingDiagnostics.block_length is None for it.

    • A model can hold bins of different units, such as people and the concentration of bacteria in water: each bin starts at an amount in its own unit, a model whose bins all start at 0 builds and runs, and the Calibrator rejects an initial-condition remainder whose unit is not the unit of a calibrated amount. See Bins of different units.

    • Sensitivity analysis with no data: SensitivityAnalysis(simulation, inputs, replicates=None, seed=None).run(method), the third entry point of the registry, with methods that subclass SensitivityMethod[R]. What varies is an InputSpace of calibration parameters (model parameters or initial conditions): independent distributions (InputSpace.independent, InputSpace.uniform_on_bounds) or a weighted sample (InputSpace.from_sample). Each run is reduced in Rust to quantities of interest: ValueAt, Gain (as an observation with a window), Peak, PeakPosition and Trajectory. With a stochastic engine replicates and seed are required; a point of a design holds the mean of its replicates on common seeds, and a propagation keeps every replicate. See Sensitivity Analysis.

    • Propagation: the draws of the inputs and the quantities of each run (PropagationResult, with summary(confidence_level)); its sample gives the bands of a Predictive, which runs the same replicates.
    • PRCC: partial rank correlation coefficients and their p-values (PRCCResult), weighted for a sample, with Kish's effective size in the test.
    • Morris: elementary effects, mu, mu_star and sigma (MorrisResult), on levels at the centres of strata of equal probability.
    • Sobol: first-order and total indices on the design of Saltelli, with bootstrap intervals (SobolResult).
    • PolynomialChaos: a Legendre expansion of F(x), its leave-one-out error, its Sobol indices and predict as a surrogate (ChaosResult); the number of terms is checked before any run.
    • SimulationPlotter.plot_prcc, plot_morris and plot_sobol.
    • ProfileLikelihood(parameters, threshold, max_steps): the profile of each parameter, refitted with the problem's own optimizer at values that step away from the fit by the adaptive step of Raue et al. (2009), its pointwise interval and its verdict (identifiable, practically_non_identifiable, structurally_non_identifiable), in a ProfileResult; ProfileResult.report() gives the verdicts as an IdentifiabilityReport with a profile section. A profile is read by resetting the other parameters to their fitted values (ProfileResult.structure), against the floor of two evaluations (ProfileResult.evaluation_floor): it gives the structural verdict in_combination or non_identifiable, and names the parameters that compensate it. A crossing made by the bound of another parameter counts as an open side (Profile.box_crossings), and Profile.refit_error measures the error of a refit at the fit. See Profile Likelihood.
    • PredictionProfile(specs, threshold): the interval of each prediction (PredictionSpec), held at each value as an equality constraint by an augmented Lagrangian. See Prediction profiles.
    • Threshold.chi_square(level) (observation models), Threshold.beale(level) (a sum of squares of independent Gaussian errors) and Threshold.bootstrap_calibrated(bootstrap, level) (any loss, calibrated by the replicates of a bootstrap).
    • SimulationPlotter.plot_profiles.

    Changed¶

    • Nelder-Mead with an infinite bound fails with the message Nelder-Mead needs finite bounds, instead of a rate that is not a number. The sine transform of the bounds and the root finder of the quantiles come from the argmin fork; calibrations, profiles, quantiles and draws give the same results.
    • Breaking: InformationMatchingEta(block_length) takes a required block_length, an int or "auto": the variance of the scores is summed over blocks of positions, as the meat of Sandwich, so it holds under dependence; block_length=1 gives the independent version. It also takes a problem with observation models, where a correct model gives η ≈ 1.
    • Breaking: Predictive(source, sample, positions) takes a Calibrator or a SensitivityAnalysis (any PredictiveSource), and its attribute calibrator is now source. Predictive.at_observations of an analysis takes only NoNoise(), and scores.log_score refuses its predictive.
    • With a stochastic engine, every replicate of every draw of a Predictive, of Predictive.at_observations and of scores.log_score has a seed of its own, derived from the calibrator's seed; before, every draw ran the same replicates of the problem, so a predictive with few replicates showed the same few noise paths for every draw. The bands and scores of a stochastic predictive change; a seed still repeats them.
    • Breaking: ModelStructure.check_calibration_values(model, engine, parameters, remainder) takes the values and the remainder instead of a calibration problem; a model kind that implements it changes its signature.
    • A prior whose family does not fit its bounds is named "The distribution ... of" in its message, instead of "The prior ... of".
    • The starts of Calibrator.run(starts=n) come from the Latin hypercube of commol-random; a seed gives the same starts as before.
    • Breaking: an Aggregate states the initial amount of each bin in the unit of the bin, not fractions of one total. A structure that states total, bin_fractions or compartment_fractions is rejected with the fields to use.

    • Breaking: a calibrated initial condition of a bin, a bin prefix or a compartment is an amount in the unit of its bin, not a fraction of the total; its bounds are amounts too. A category value is still a split. The remainder takes the sum of the amounts the model states less every other compartment, so a bin that no value covers keeps its amount; with a bin left None, the remainder holds the sum ({"S": 1000, "I": None}). Without a remainder no initial state is infeasible. A remainder below zero is measured as a share of the amounts the values set. The write-back updates the amount of a bin only when the values and the remainder set every compartment of it.
    • Breaking: applied_parameters_report gives an aggregate model the sections initial_amounts (amount, unit, calibrated) and category_splits (group, a list of categories, fraction, calibrated) in place of initial_conditions, with no total row. A category split is reported as the fraction of its group, with no rescaling of its interval.
    • Breaking: a stochastic engine needs a whole amount in each bin, not a whole total, and largest_remainder and random round within each bin, so a unit never moves to another bin.
    • Breaking: the description of an aggregate model shows the bin amounts and their units, and the initial state with the unit of each compartment.
    • Breaking (Rust): InitialConditions has bin_amounts and compartment_amounts in place of total, bin_fractions and compartment_fractions; CompartmentalEngine::total is the sum of the amounts the model states; check_model_values returns the plan it resolves, and InitialConditionPlan::coverage gives the compartments each value sets.
    • Breaking: CalibrationProblem.loss_function has no default: a problem needs loss_function or observation_models, exactly one, and raises a ValueError with both or neither. Write loss_function="sse" where a problem relied on the default.
    • Breaking: public class names write every letter of an acronym in capitals, as PEP 8 asks: OdeSolver is ODESolver. EnbPI keeps the spelling of its authors. See Conventions.

    Fixed¶

    • A DifferentialEquations run with method="tsit45" whose solution blows up returned NaN with no error. A step that leaves the state not finite now raises RuntimeError that names the last position the solver reached, with either method.
    • The finite-difference step of a parameter with an infinite bound was infinite, so its sensitivities, identifiability and curvature failed: the width of its bounds now counts as 1 in the step.
    • The quantile of a distribution hundreds of orders of magnitude below 1, such as Beta(0.01, 1) at 1e-3, was far from it, so a prior or a draw of such a shape was wrong: when its Newton and halving steps run out, the quantile now bisects the floating-point numbers between the two ends.
    • A particle swarm whose first particle had a NaN cost kept no best particle and returned its start point: a NaN cost now counts as the worst.
    • A transition whose rate holds $compartment dropped per_compartment, accumulators and the absolute of its stratified rates: each transition it expands to now keeps them, so with per_compartment=True $compartment reads the compartment that flows, and its accumulators count the flow.
    • A Calibrator on a Simulation that had run shared a calibrated bin, bin prefix or remainder across its compartments in the proportions of the last state of the run, so its fit, its uncertainty and its Predictive started from a wrong split: they now share it in the proportions of the initial state.
    • Model.update_parameters wrote each value with no check, so a list of points for a series parameter left a model that Simulation could not read, with an error that named no parameter. It now takes a list of points for a series parameter and keeps its mode, or a SeriesValue (now in commol) with a new mode, and it runs the checks of a new model: a value it cannot take raises ValueError at the call, with the id of the parameter, and leaves the model unchanged.
    • A Predictive at positions out of order failed inside a draw with a RuntimeError that named a parameter set: it now raises ValueError before any draw runs, with the first pair out of order, as it does for a position the engine rejects.

    [0.5.0] - 2026-10-19¶

    This release replaces the ensemble calibration with uncertainty methods that have a stated meaning. Calibrator.uncertainty resamples the data (bootstraps and the jackknife) and returns a ParameterSample; a Predictive runs its draws, and commol.scores scores its bands. Calibrator.identifiability tells which parameters the model and the data determine, and Calibrator.run(starts=n) fits from several start points. Before you upgrade, read the Breaking entries: the ensemble calibration is removed (use BayesianBootstrap in its place), and every interval uses one quantile, so the intervals of member_statistics and SimulationReplicates.quantiles move slightly. See Uncertainty and Resampling.

    Added¶

    • Calibrator.run(starts=n) runs the optimizer from n start points in parallel and returns the best: the initial guess, then rows of a Latin hypercube over the bounds drawn from the problem's seed. CalibrationResult.starts lists every start (StartSummary: start, end, loss, converged); distinct ends with equal losses show that the data do not identify the parameters. See Several starts.
    • CalibrationResult.failed_runs: why each failed start failed, also logged as a warning.
    • ParameterSample: weighted draws of the parameters of a problem, with their invariants checked at construction and weighted statistics computed in Rust (mean, covariance, correlation, quantiles, interval, effective_size, draws, resample, summary). It saves to JSON and loads back equal.
    • Predictive(calibrator, sample, positions): the run of every draw, in parallel, its band (band, series_band) and every draw's value of a set of observations through the observation operator of the loss, with an observation noise that has no default (at_observations(observed_data, noise): NoNoise() for a band of the model, ResidualNoise(...) for a prediction interval). SimulationPlotter and simulation_report take a predictive.
    • commol.scores: empirical coverage, interval score, weighted interval score, the CRPS of a weighted sample and PIT values of a predictive, per observation and per series. Each takes the observation noise, so a band of the model is never scored as a prediction interval by accident.
    • Calibrator.uncertainty(method): one entry point for every uncertainty method. A method's configuration subclasses UncertaintyMethod[R] and registers its runner; the result type follows the configuration's type.
    • Calibrator.identifiability(method): which parameters the model and the data determine, and which combinations they determine in their place. StructuralRank (the rank of the sensitivities at random points, on the design of the observations or a dense one), Collinearity (Brun's indices at a fit or over a sample), SampleIdentifiability (contraction, correlations and modes of a sample) and StartsIdentifiability (starts of equal loss that end apart). Each returns an IdentifiabilityReport with a structural and a practical verdict per parameter; merge joins reports and identifiability_report gives one as plain data. See Identifiability.
    • InferenceMethod[R], the common base of UncertaintyMethod[R] and IdentifiabilityMethod[R]; each entry point refuses the other kind with a TypeError.
    • weighted_quantiles, and a weights argument for member_statistics.
    • CalibrationHistory and CalibrationEvaluation, returned by Calibrator.run_with_history().
    • The crate commol-inference: the parameter sample, the one quantile, the predictive and the scores. See Uncertainty.
    • Resampling methods with no noise model, through Calibrator.uncertainty: BayesianBootstrap, ResidualBootstrap and WildBootstrap return a BootstrapResult (the fit, one draw per replicate, diagnostics, and the Monte Carlo error of each interval bound, interval_error); BlockJackknife returns a JackknifeResult (estimate, bias, standard error, pseudo-values and the influence of each block). Blocks of consecutive positions span every series; block_length is an integer or "auto" (Politis and White). See Resampling.
    • ResidualNoise: observation noise made of blocks of residuals of a fit, which gives a prediction interval with no noise model; NoNoise gives a band of the model.
    • CalibrationProblem::with_data (Rust): a copy of a problem with other observed values and weights.

    Changed¶

    • The latest version of the documentation site is the documentation of the last release, published from its tag; the dev version follows main.
    • Breaking: every interval uses one quantile: the weighted inverse of the empirical distribution between the midpoints of the cumulative weights, NumPy's method="hazen" with equal weights. member_statistics and SimulationReplicates.quantiles used NumPy's linear; their intervals move slightly.
    • Breaking: CalibrationResult.best_parameters keeps the order of CalibrationProblem.parameters.
    • Breaking: Calibrator.run_with_history() returns a CalibrationHistory instead of the extension's object.
    • Breaking: applied_parameters_report(..., sample=, confidence_level=) reads the statistics of a ParameterSample; parameter_statistics is gone.
    • Breaking (Rust): CalibrationProblem is split into layers (ForwardModel, ObservationOperator, Feasibility, Discrepancy, OptimizationObjective); optimize takes the problem by reference, and the optimizer configurations have no verbose field. CalibrationEvaluation has no predictions.
    • A verbose optimizer writes its table to Python's sys.stdout only, and needs one start; the library never writes to the standard error.
    • A rate, a formula parameter or a constraint that the expression compiler cannot compile, such as typeof(k), is rejected when the model or the calibration problem is built, with the reason; it no longer fails when it is evaluated.
    • The functions erf, erfc, tgamma, lgamma, j0, j1, y0, y1, fdim and remainder of a rate come from the libm crate, so they give the same values on every platform; the other functions still come from the system's math library.
    • AxisPattern is abstract: AxisPattern() raises TypeError. Build a pattern with one of its class methods.
    • Error messages that list ids (the stratifications of a transition, the missing and extra keys of the fractions of an Aggregate) list them in sorted order.

    Fixed¶

    • Several starts of Nelder-Mead began at the same point: each start now comes from the run seed.
    • A parameter value could land on another parameter when results were read by name from an unordered mapping.
    • A failed run of a multi-start optimization was written to the standard error and dropped; it is now part of the result.
    • An error of the model at a vertex of the initial Nelder-Mead simplex made the optimizer panic; it now stops the calibration with its message (the argmin fork returns the error).
    • A to condition on a transition without a source was ignored by its flow but applied by its accumulators; build now rejects it, and a category condition chooses the compartment the flow enters.
    • Model.from_json read a file in the encoding of the system; it now reads UTF-8, the encoding of JSON.
    • Model.check_unit_consistency checked every flow of a category transition in the unit of the first bin of the model; each flow is now checked in the unit of its own bin. In a model of bins of different units, a rate that reads total, a subgroup total, a category or neighbors fails the check: these add amounts of different units.
    • The LaTeX description wrote a part of a rate in parentheses, such as (I / N) or the argument of sin(t / T), as upright text, and wrote a unit inside the name of another variable (A_{g1(\text{item})}); only units are text now, each after its variable. The expanded form merges k * p and k * (1 - p) also when a third term shares another factor, and a merged term keeps the place of its first term.
    • ModelBuilder and Simulation logged to the root logger of the application; they log to the commol logger, as the rest of the library does, so add_stderr_logger shows their messages.
    • AxisPattern.combine(mode="max") raised "Unknown combine mode": a mode may be given by its value.
    • A quantile level outside [0, 1], or NaN, gave the smallest or the largest value; ParameterSample.quantiles and weighted_quantiles now raise ValueError.
    • AxisPattern.from_formula and AxisPattern.combine with a config that allows formulas longer than the default rejected them anyway; the configuration now applies.
    • A rate of StochasticSteps that is not a number raised InfeasibleStateError and said that a rate cannot be negative; it now raises RuntimeError, as StochasticEvents does, and says that the rate is not a number.
    • A rate that is not a number gave amounts that are not numbers in DifferenceEquations and DifferentialEquations, and a calibration only penalized the run; the run now stops with RuntimeError that names the flow and the position, as in the stochastic engines.
    • A rate that read an id with digits, such as e1, E2 or c1star, was rejected as a malformed number; and the exponent of a number such as 1e1 was read as the undefined variable e1. An identifier now starts only after a character that is not a letter, a digit or _, so the digits of an id belong to its name and an exponent belongs to its number.

    Removed¶

    • commol.api.model_loader.ModelLoader: a copy of Model.from_json that no page documented and nothing called.
    • Breaking: the ensemble calibration: Calibrator.run_probabilistic, ProbabilisticCalibrator, CalibrationProblem.probabilistic_config, ProbabilisticCalibrationConfig, ProbabilisticCalibrationResult, EnsembleSolution, EnsembleSelectionSummary, ParameterSetStatistics and their settings classes. Its intervals had no statistical meaning: its members were points the optimizer visited, and the selection maximized the coverage of the data it was fitted to. The closest replacement with no noise model is Calibrator.uncertainty(BayesianBootstrap(replicates=..., block_length=...)): the same parallel fits, each on randomly weighted data, with a stated meaning.
    • The dependency on scikit-learn.

    [0.4.0] - 2026-10-16¶

    Added¶

    • Model.describe(layout="default") returns a ModelDescription: the model as data, sections of tables, equations, state graphs and text, for every kind of model. render(format) gives text, Markdown (with the state graph as a Mermaid diagram), a LaTeX document, JSON or Graphviz DOT, and save(path) writes it in the format of the suffix (.txt, .md, .tex, .json, .dot). In a notebook it shows as Markdown. See Describing a Model.
    • layout="odd": the description follows the ODD protocol for describing individual- and agent-based models (Grimm et al. 2006, 2010, 2020), and marks what commol cannot fill "To be written by the modeller.".
    • EngineType.dynamics_form and EngineType.schedule: how an engine advances (difference_equation, differential_equation, stochastic_events, rules), as a description shows it.
    • The process of a model apart from its structure: Model.process (Process: bins, accumulators, stratifications, transitions) is written once, and Model.structure lays it out. Aggregate is the structure of amounts per compartment, the kind aggregate.
    • neighbors_<bin> and neighbors in a transition rate: the amount of a bin among the neighbors of a unit and their total. A rate that reads them and no compartment is proportional. Aggregate(neighbors=Complete()) makes every unit a neighbor of every other; Groups(stratification, weights) makes units neighbors by the categories of a stratification. A rate that reads them needs neighbors, with no default.
    • Variants of a structure: a subclass with the same kind, fields and engines, saved with its variant and loaded back as its own class.
    • Seeds and replicates for stochastic engines: Simulation.run(…, seed=) and run_at(…, seed=) (SimulationResults.seed keeps the seed, drawn when none is given), Simulation.replicates(n, seed=) with run and run_at that return SimulationReplicates (runs in parallel, one seed each; mean, quantiles, member_statistics), Simulation.is_stochastic, and EngineType.is_stochastic. SimulationPlotter draws the mean and a band of replicates, and simulation_report takes them as ensemble_runs.
    • CalibrationProblem(replicates=…, replicate_summary=…) calibrates a stochastic engine with common random numbers: "mean", "median", Quantile(q) or "crps" (the continuous ranked probability score). A stochastic engine needs them and a seed; there is no default summary.
    • The crate commol-random: a portable generator (Xoshiro256PlusPlus), the derivation of replicate seeds, samplers and a sum tree of rates, for every stochastic engine.
    • Two stochastic engines of an aggregate model, in the crate commol-stochastic, that run the process of any model as whole units moved at random, with no change to it: "StochasticEvents" (continuous time: Gillespie's direct method, exact also when a rate reads t, or method="tau_leaping") and "StochasticSteps" (the chain binomial, with per-step probabilities). Their settings are StochasticEventsSettings and StochasticStepsSettings. A total must be whole, and initial amounts that are not whole need rounding="largest_remainder" or "random", with no default. A per-step probability outside [0, 1], or probabilities out of one compartment that sum to more than 1, raise InfeasibleStateError. See Stochastic Simulation.
    • The dynamics section of a stochastic model is an event table: each flow, the change of one event and its propensity or its draw; the schedule section lists the settings of the engine.

    Changed¶

    [0.2.0] - 2026-10-12¶

    Added¶

    • Per-compartment initial conditions: InitialConditions.compartment_fractions (Python and Rust) accepts overrides keyed by expanded compartment name, expressed as fractions of the total population and applied after the uniform expansion. Includes ModelBuilder.set_initial_conditions(compartment_fractions=...), Model.update_compartment_fractions, and Model.initial_state(), which returns each expanded compartment with its bin, categories and initial population as the engine builds it. The Rust engine is the single source of truth for the expansion and validates the overrides (unknown or repeated name, fraction outside 0.0..=1.0, total above population_size).
    • Rust commol_difference::initial_state and commol_calibration::calibrated_initial_population, exposed to Python. The calibration objective, the calibrated predictions and the model write-back share one implementation of how calibration values reach the engine.

    Changed¶

    • Breaking: calibrated initial conditions no longer auto-calculate the last population-fraction value as the remainder of the population. The new CalibrationProblem.initial_condition_remainder names the compartment or bin that absorbs the difference; it is not calibrated and keeps its own split. Without it, values are applied as given and only an initial population above population_size is penalized. A single exact compartment, such as a composite state, can now be calibrated on its own.
    • Breaking: calibrated initial conditions are applied in fixed layers — bins, category splits, exact compartments, remainder — instead of in parameter order.
    • Breaking: a stratification category may be named with a qualified id (sex:female). A bare name that more than one stratification declares is rejected as ambiguous, in calibration and in update_stratification_fractions, and the report qualifies its rows. Category pairs come from the engine's compartment labels instead of parsing compartment names.
    • Breaking: get_category_fraction, subgroup_population and get_conditioning_categories resolve category ids like calibration: a qualified id (sex:female) selects its stratification and a bare name that more than one stratification declares raises instead of silently using the first one. get_conditioning_categories returns qualified ids for shared names.
    • Bin and bin-prefix initial-condition ids, and the remainder, resolve from the engine's compartment labels instead of name prefixes, so a bin A no longer covers the compartments of a bin A_x.
    • The Rust engine expands bins and accumulators in one function; the equation printer and unit checker use it (expanded_outputs) instead of their own Python expansion. The Rust ModelTypes gains DifferentialEquations, so any model definition can be expanded; the difference engine rejects it.
    • Breaking: update_bin_fractions / update_initial_conditions apply the same rule as construction: the bin fractions must sum to 1.0 after the update.

    • Breaking (Rust): SimulationEngine requires compartment_labels(). CalibrationProblem::fix_auto_calculated_parameters and get_parameter_fix_info are removed; applied_parameter_values returns the clamped values the objective scored, which is what results now report.

    • Breaking: transitions are planned by the Rust engine from compartment labels (bin and categories) instead of compartment names, in one planner shared by the simulation and print_equations. The printed equations are therefore the simulated ones: a per-capita rate is shown multiplied by its source (gamma * I), a transition without flows shows (no flows), and the compact form lists the flows of each transition.

    • Breaking: building a model rejects a stratified rate whose category conditions match no source compartment (use to to route into a stratification the source does not carry), a transition with several sources and a per-capita rate, a category transition without exactly one source and one target, and a flow that ends in categories no compartment has. These used to be skipped without notice.
    • Constraint aggregates (a bin or bin prefix in a constraint expression) are built from output labels, so a bin A no longer sums the outputs of a bin A_x.

    Fixed¶

    • A transition between bins no longer moves bins whose id starts with the source bin id: A -> B moved AB -> BB as well.
    • Transitions between the categories of a stratification (young -> old) are now simulated; the engine used to ignore them while print_equations showed them.

    • A transition with several sources or targets (A + B -> 2 C) now applies its flow to every source and target, as documented; the engine used to move only the first source to the first target.

    • The applied-parameters report now shows the effective head count for bin, stratification-category, and subgroup rows — the uniform expansion plus the declared compartment_fractions overrides — and reports each fraction as that head count over the declared population_size, so every row shares one denominator with N. An aggregate row written back as overrides no longer reports the stale pre-calibration value as calibrated, and a bin left uncalibrated keeps its null marker even when overrides touch some of its compartments.

    • A conditional-stratification category is calibrated as a fraction of its conditioning subgroup but reported as a fraction of the whole population, so the report rescales its interval by the subgroup's share of the population; the interval now brackets the reported fraction.
    • apply_calibration_parameters writes initial-condition values back so the model rebuilds the initial population the engine scored for them. Bin and category values update the fractions they declare, as the engine applied them; every compartment those fractions do not reproduce is pinned as a per-compartment override. The call computes the result before it changes the model, so a rejected value leaves the model unchanged.
    • population_size must be greater than zero, reported as a domain ValueError rather than a later ZeroDivisionError. update_bin_fractions re-applies the sum-to-1/conservation rule.
    • Calibrating a bare category name that two stratifications declare no longer builds an initial population larger than population_size: the engine used to split the overlapping pairs of both stratifications from one snapshot.
    • The PyO3 InitialConditions binding exposes population_size, bin_fractions, stratification_fractions and compartment_fractions with getters, and the _commol_rs stub declares only those real attributes (correcting the bin_fractions field name).
    • An initial-condition override that names an unknown compartment raises a ValueError when the model is built or updated, and a RuntimeError from the DifferenceEquations engine constructor.
    • The greedy ensemble search of probabilistic calibration takes its random starts from the shuffled part of the sample (argmin fork). The starts were biased towards low indices, so the same seed can now select a different ensemble.
