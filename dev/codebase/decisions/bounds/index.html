    Fits near a bound¶

    Back to Design decisions

    What the local approximations and information matching do with a fit close to a bound of a parameter. A fit on a bound is refused by the rule of Curvature; the methods are on Local approximations.

    A bound within 3 sd of the fit is a note¶

    Gaussian::near_bounds (crates/commol-inference/src/local/gaussian.rs) gives each bound whose distance from the centre is below NEAR_BOUND = 3 standard deviations, read on the scale of the parameters, as a NearBound. One formatter, NearBound::head, words the parameter, the distance and the bound. Gaussian::bound_notes adds what the draws of Laplace and Sandwich show and the pointer to a profile likelihood, and the binding appends them to the notes of the curvature (PyLocalRun::of, py-commol/src/local.rs). laplace_mixture adds the end of the component and its own consequence: the importance weights correct the skew as far as k̂ says, and with a bound at 0.49 sd its weighted 90 % interval is [9.638, 10.080] against the exact [9.628, 10.080] (0.05 sd: [9.613, 9.997] against [9.604, 9.997]), so it points to no profile. The runners of Python log every note as a warning.

    The scale is (dθ/du)_j √Σ_jj with Σ the covariance that the draws read: H_u⁻¹ for Laplace, (s·H_u)⁻¹ for a component of the mixture, and the sandwich for the sandwich, whose grouped t it scales. With σ = √Σ_jj and the centre u on the unbounded scale, the distance d in that scale is (1 + eᵘ)/σ from the lower bound of a logit, (1 + e⁻ᵘ)/σ from its upper bound and 1/σ from the bound of a log: no product with dθ/du, which rounds near a bound. A parameter with no bound has none. A normal reads d; a Student t of ν_j degrees of freedom reads z = Φ⁻¹(1 − P(T_ν > d)), at most d, where a normal leaves the mass that the t leaves past the bound, and an infinite ν is a normal. So the rule is the mass past the bound, 0.135 % at 3 sd, whatever the tails. The words give the distance with two decimals, never rounded up to 3, or two digits below 0.01, and say when it is read from a t. Only a fit within ON_BOUND of its bound is refused.

    The covariance and delta_band describe the fit at any distance from a bound; the draws do not. A flat prior and a normal likelihood cut by the bound give a 90 % interval of the draws 1.05, 1.18, 1.94 and 6.0 times the width of the exact one at 3, 2, 1 and 0.5 sd, whose far end sits at 2.19, 2.55, 4.18 and 12.9 sd, where the exact one sits at 1.65 to 1.82. A natural bound, a Poisson rate at 0, gives 1.01, 1.02 and 1.14 times at 3, 2 and 1 sd. Of the 33 fits of Laplace and Sandwich in the tests and the guide, 8 have a bound within 3 sd, and none within 1.

    Alternative Why not
    A refusal below some number of sd The covariance and the band hold, and a natural bound skews the draws little at 1 sd: the reader judges from the note.
    A threshold of 1 or 2 sd At 2 sd the interval is already 1.18 times as wide.
    A share of the width of the bounds, as ON_BOUND It says where an optimizer that the bound stops ends: a fit at 1e-6 of the width has draws over the whole width.
    √(H_θ⁻¹)_jj for the sandwich With a loss it is no variance, and the draws read the sandwich.
    The scale √Σ_jj of the t as its sd It under-notes with few degrees of freedom: at ν = 1 a bound 3.27 units away has 9.4 % past it, and at ν = 2 one 4.46 units away 2.3 %, with no note, while the 99 % interval of the draws spans the whole width.
    The variance of the t, σ² ν/(ν − 2) It has no value for ν ≤ 2, jumps near 2, and still under-notes.
    One wording for every method The weighted sample of the mixture is near the exact one: its draws do not spread far, and a profile is no remedy it needs.
    The check in Curvature The curvature does not know the covariance that is drawn, which the sandwich and the curvature scale of a component change.
    Truncated or reflected draws They change the distribution that the covariance describes.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_fit_within_a_few_sd_of_a_bound_is_noted, tests/uncertainty/test_fit_near_a_bound.py::TestLaplace, tests/uncertainty/test_fit_near_a_bound.py::TestSandwich, tests/uncertainty/test_fit_near_a_bound.py::TestLaplaceMixture, crates/commol-inference/src/local/gaussian.rs::a_distance_in_sd_never_reads_as_the_threshold, tests/uncertainty/test_local.py::TestCurvature::test_a_fit_on_its_bound_points_to_a_profile.

    Information matching reads the curvature on the scale of the parameters¶

    information_matching_eta (smc/gibbs.rs) takes its curvature from Curvature::gauss_newton_on_the_parameter_scale: the Gauss–Newton curvature with the identity transform, whose scales are powers of two and which refuses no fit on a bound. It reads no covariance on the unbounded scale, only traces of matrices in their units, and those do not read the transform: inside the bounds η is the one that the curvature of Curvature::gauss_newton gives, and on a bound it is a number. There is no note. When the optimum lies past the bound, the scores of the fit do not sum to 0, so the trace is too large and η too small: with 24 values of sd 1 in blocks of 3 and 4000 data sets, the median of η is 1.03, 0.86, 0.62, 0.41 and 0.20, where 1 is right, with the optimum 0, 1, 2, 3 and 5 sd past the bound. The guide says so.

    Alternative Why not
    Refuse a fit on a bound, as the local approximations do tr(J⁻¹ I) does not depend on the scale of a parameter: the refusal reads a variance that η does not use.
    Skip the check and keep the unbounded scale A scale of 0 on the bound, which every reader of Curvature::scales would have to guard.
    A note of η near a bound η holds near a bound that does not cut the optimum, and the fit alone does not say whether it cuts it.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_smc.rs::information_matching_gives_eta_at_a_fit_near_or_on_its_bound, tests/uncertainty/test_fit_near_a_bound.py::TestInformationMatching.
