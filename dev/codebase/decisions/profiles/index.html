    Profiles¶

    Back to Design decisions

    How a profile steps away from the fit, which threshold reads its statistic, and when its shape gives a verdict.

    A profile steps with the adaptive step of Raue¶

    ProfileTracer (crates/commol-inference/src/profiles/tracer.rs) walks each side of each quantity from the fit, all in parallel. It knows no optimizer and no threshold rule: a Subproblem holds the quantity, and the caller gives the threshold. The first step is 1 % of the width of the bounds (of |value at the fit| for a prediction); a step that rises by more than 2 q T is rejected and halved, one that rises by less than q T / 2 doubles the next (q = 0.1, T the threshold, at least the tolerance). Each refit starts from every parameter, and the multiplier of a held prediction, extrapolated along the line through the two points before. A side ends past the threshold, at a bound, after max_steps points, or at the fifth miss of a held prediction (20 outer iterations of the augmented Lagrangian that leave |g − c| above a thousandth of the step). The end of the interval is the root of the quadratic through the last three points of the side, or of the line through the last two.

    Alternative Why not
    A fixed grid Too coarse near the fit or too slow far from it, and blind to jumps.
    Integration of the profile path (Chen and Jennrich 2002) It needs exact derivatives (#135).
    Only the ends of the interval (Venzon and Moolgavkar 1988) No trace for the verdicts and the plots.
    The previous point as the start More iterations on a curved ridge, for no saving.
    The line through the last two points as the end Its error on a parabola is a share of the step; the quadratic is exact there.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_profiles.rs::profile_of_a_linear_model_is_a_parabola, tests/uncertainty/test_profiles.py::test_linear_normal_profiles_are_exact.

    A threshold states the noise¶

    Threshold has no default. chi_square gives ½ χ²₁(level) on −log L and refuses a loss; beale gives S(θ̂) · F(1, n − p; level) / (n − p) and needs the loss "sse"; bootstrap_calibrated calibrates the statistic itself (prepivoting, Beran 1988): replicate b of the bootstrap, rebuilt from replicate_seed(seed, b) as ReplicateRunner::run builds it, is fitted and then fitted again with the quantity held at its value at the original fit, and the threshold is the quantile level of the rises (bootstrap_thresholds, threshold.rs).

    Alternative Why not
    The profile of the original data at the values of the replicates Not a pivot: it gives the coverage of the percentile bootstrap, and a threshold near 0 on a flat profile.
    One threshold from L(θ̂_b) − L(θ̂) It calibrates a joint region, not a pointwise interval.
    Only chi_square It drops every problem with a loss.
    Only beale Exact only with independent Gaussian errors.

    Guards: tests/uncertainty/test_profiles.py (TestThresholds), tests/uncertainty/test_profiles_coverage.py.

    A verdict needs a threshold above the tolerance¶

    Profile::verdict (verdict.rs) reads the ends and the statistics of a profile, at the tolerance ε = max(τ, ν) (ProfileTracer::tolerance). τ is the tolerance of the optimizer: the sd_tolerance of Nelder-Mead as an absolute amount, since argmin stops when the sample standard deviation of the costs of the simplex falls below it; for a swarm, 1e-6 · max(1, |f̂|), provisional until it has stop rules (#230). ν is the noise floor: the error of one evaluation of the objective at the fit, propagated from the accuracy of a run, Σ |∂ℓᵢ/∂mᵢ| · eᵢ with eᵢ = rtol · |mᵢ| + k · atol for an observation that sums k outputs, the term derivatives of CalibrationProblem::term_derivatives and the output_accuracy and output_absolute_accuracy of the engine (ProfileTracer::noise_floor); solvers hold their tolerances per step, so ν is an estimate, not a bound. A point whose refit has a free parameter on a bound, within ON_BOUND = 1e-8 of the width of its bounds (the tolerance of #133), that it is not on at the fit has left the box of the parameters (ProfilePoint::on_bound): the bound, not the data, makes its rise. structurally_non_identifiable: each side is flat within ε over its points inside the box, has one past the fit, and ends at a bound, an unreachable prediction or a point outside the box; the result notes where the ridge leaves the box (Profile::box_exits). identifiable: otherwise, both sides cross. practically_non_identifiable: the rest. A crossing whose point has a free parameter on a bound it is not on over the whole side from the fit comes from the box (Profile::box_crossings): the side counts as a bound end, and the interval end stays with a note. A parameter that stays on its bound from the fit to the crossing is not seen, so the verdict holds within the box. A threshold at or below ε gives no verdict, and the result says why. With a stochastic engine a flat profile reads practically_non_identifiable (ProfileTracer::verdict).

    Alternative Why not
    A verdict from a threshold of 0 A rise of numerical error would read as information.
    τ scaled by max(1, |f̂|) argmin compares the spread of the costs, an absolute amount; a −log L far from 0 would hide real rises.
    Every point, outside the box too A ridge k1 · s = c leaves a box unless its bounds match c exactly, so a flat ridge would read as practically non-identifiable.
    Any parameter on a bound A parameter whose fit sits on its bound would put every point outside the box, and any profile would read as flat.
    A box exit before the crossing demotes it A ridge that leaves the box and then rises through the data would lose a correct identifiable.

    Guards: verdict::tests, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_profile_structure.rs (the_noise_floor_propagates_the_accuracy_of_a_run, a_threshold_at_or_below_the_tolerance_reads_no_structure), tests/uncertainty/test_profile_verdicts.py (TestShapes).

    A flat profile is read by resetting the others¶

    A profile is flat because other parameters compensate the parameter along it, or because nothing the data read depends on it; both satisfy "not determined alone", and Raue et al. (2009) read relations from the paths of the profiles. ProfileTracer::read_structure (resets.rs) tells them apart with no optimizer: the parameter is set to each value of its profile inside the box and to both ends of its box, the others to their fitted values, and the objective, evaluated once, is compared with the objective at the fit f̂, feasibility from Score.feasible; an evaluation that fails is an infeasible reset. Two evaluations differ by up to the evaluation floor 2 · (ν₁ + ν₂) + 2 · γₙ · Σ |ℓᵢ| (ProfileTracer::evaluation_floor): ν₁ is ν, ν₂ = Σ ½ |ℓᵢ''| eᵢ² from the expected curvature of TermDerivatives (an estimate for a model that is not normal), and γₙ = n·u / (1 − n·u) bounds the rounding of the sum of the n terms, constants of −log L included. With no floor, as for the mean absolute error or the root mean squared error of a perfect fit, there is no structural verdict, with a note.

    Every reset feasible and within the floor of f̂ gives non_identifiable, unless a free parameter sits on its bound at the fit; since the minimum of the profile is at most each reset, this proves the profile flat from above, and its own verdict reads structurally_non_identifiable. A profile flat within ε with a feasible reset inside the box above f̂ + T gives in_combination; the ends of the box only rule out non_identifiable. Below the fit by more than the floor, with a stochastic engine, or otherwise, a flat profile gets no structural verdict, the report gives the practical poorly_determined, and a note says why. A parameter is named in the reason when resetting it alone, at its largest displacement on the scale of Transform, raises the objective of that point above T; the parameters not named, noise parameters included, are then reset together on each side, and a rise above the floor (two evaluations) makes the list "may be incomplete". combinations keeps the algebraic combinations of the structural rank. Every verdict holds with the other parameters at their fitted values. The error of a refit at the fit, |δ| (ProfileTracer::refit_error), is a diagnostic only, with a note when it is above ε: the flatness keeps ε, and a too small ε falls back to the practical verdict.

    Conflicts with StructuralRank remain, and the merge shows them as conflicting: the rank reads the generic rank over a Latin hypercube across the box, a profile one ridge through the fit; the rank cuts the projection on the null space at 0.95 and 0.05, so in k1 + k2 with k1 ≫ k2 it calls k2 non-identifiable while its profile reads in_combination; and a parameter with no effect at the fit of the others but an effect elsewhere reads non_identifiable for the profile.

    Alternative Why not
    A flat profile is always non_identifiable It conflicts with the rank on every product of parameters.
    The parameters that move along the profile A parameter nothing reads moves by chance, and its noise reads as a combination.
    A reset at every point for every parameter It multiplies the cost of a profile by the parameters.
    Each reset against the profile at the same point The profile carries the error of the optimizer; f̂ and the reset are two evaluations, whose difference the floor bounds.
    |δ| as the tolerance Two samples of the error of the optimizer are no bound on it.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_profile_structure.rs, tests/uncertainty/test_profile_verdicts.py (TestStructure, TestReport).
