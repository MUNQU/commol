    Curvature¶

    Back to Design decisions

    How the curvature of the objective at a fit is read, and inverted, for the methods of Local approximations and for information matching.

    The curvature lives on the unbounded scale¶

    Curvature (crates/commol-inference/src/local/curvature.rs) describes H_u = D · H_θ · D, with D = dθ/du at the fit on the scale of Transform (transform.rs: a logit with two bounds, a log with one, the identity with none), centred on the fit mapped to that scale, with no curvature of the log Jacobian. It holds H_u in units of its parameters (below). Every draw maps back with from_unbounded, so it lies inside the bounds; every density on that scale, as the importance weights of the mixture, adds log_jacobian. A fit on a bound is refused with a message that names the parameter and points to a profile likelihood: its variance on that scale is infinite. The one rule of a value on a bound, on_bound in transform.rs, which the profiles also read, is 1e-8 of the width of two finite bounds, or of max(1, |bound|) with one. The tolerance comes from the optimizer: Nelder-Mead searches on a sine (SineTransform) of the unbounded coordinate, which approaches a bound quadratically in u and ends about 1e-15 of the width from it; 1e-8 of the width is a step δu ≈ 2e-4, far below any posterior spread. The mixture skips such an end (Local approximations).

    Alternative Why not
    The scale of the parameters A normal there puts mass outside the bounds near them.
    A truncated normal on the scale of the parameters It biases the mean and breaks the delta method.
    Centring on the mode of the density on the unbounded scale, Jacobian included It moves the centre away from the fit for no gain: importance sampling corrects the difference.
    A fit on a bound by exact equality An optimizer that the bound stops ends a few roundings from it, where the variance on the unbounded scale is huge but finite.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::draws_mapped_back_stay_inside_the_bounds, tests/uncertainty/test_local.py::TestCurvature::test_a_fit_on_its_bound_points_to_a_profile.

    Gauss–Newton by default, the Hessian on request¶

    Curvature::gauss_newton reads the expected curvature of each observation's term over its arguments, the mean and the noise arguments of its model (Noise::information), through the Jacobian of SensitivitySource and the identity of each noise parameter: Jᵀ W J on the model parameters, with the cross terms where they do not vanish (a log-normal of a Mean center, the two arguments of a Student t). Curvature::hessian takes the central Hessian of the objective with given steps of the finitediff crate of the argmin fork, with steps δ^{1/4} · dθ/du (δ^{1/4} · max(|θ|, 1) with no bound), δ the output accuracy of the engine, so θ ± 2h stays inside the bounds (shorter for a large curvature). Each entry of either carries an estimated error; an entry above 1 % of √(H_jj H_kk) is a note. The threshold measures the error on the scale of a correlation, so an off-diagonal entry near 0 is judged against its row and column; a note changes no result. The note gives the share only, read in the units of the parameters, since an entry on the unbounded scale can be below or above any number, and words a share above 100 % as that.

    Alternative Why not
    The Hessian always 4p² + 1 runs, and indefinite away from the optimum.
    Gauss–Newton always Wrong when the residuals are large or the mean is wrong, where the observed and expected curvature differ.
    The fixed step √(2ε) of finitediff With an ODE solved to rtol, an error of about accuracy / h².
    Differences written in commol The fork keeps the numerical methods; commol chooses the steps from its engine.

    The steps of the Hessian keep the objective a number¶

    A step δ^{1/4} (unbounded scale) puts the objective at θ ± 2h above the largest number from H_u ≈ 6e315 (δ = ε); 1/H_u is a number to 5e-324. From H_u ≈ 4e314 for that step (6e309 at δ = 1e-6; sooner for the step δ^{1/4}|θ| of no bounds) the step is the power of two at or below the one whose H_jj h_j², by the Gauss–Newton curvature in its units, is MAX / 32 (OBJECTIVE_ROOM). By |H_jk| ≤ √(H_jj H_kk) each point of the steps 2h moves the objective by at most a quarter of the largest number if the observed curvature is at most that one and the objective grows like its quadratic (not a Student t of small df); a point still not finite keeps the infeasible-point error. A MethodError::Failed that names the rounding refuses the step unless θ ± h, θ ± 2h are exact (the differences divide by the step), and unless the rounding e of the runs (run_rounding) is at most the error of the step δ^{1/4}: Σ |C| |A| e ≤ δ^{1/2} h Ĥ_jj by Gauss–Newton (rounding_of_runs).

    Alternative Why not
    Steps that move the objective by δ^{1/2} by the Gauss–Newton curvature They change every Hessian, and δ^{1/4}/√H_u is below a rounding of θ long before the objective overflows.
    The steps of δ^{1/4}, refused with words of the curvature when a point is not finite It refuses a covariance that is a number, which Gauss–Newton gives.
    Halve the steps until every point is finite Up to 4p² + 1 runs for each halving, when the bound is known before any run.
    A floor of δ^{1/2} times the size of θ on the step It reads the origin of θ: it refuses a parameter far from 0 whose runs are those of one near it, and accepts a large constant in the runs.
    Exact points only The rounding of the runs then gives wrong values with no note.
    A note in place of the refusal When the rounding makes the Hessian indefinite, the error still says that the data do not determine the parameters.

    The curvature refuses a prediction or a derivative that is not finite¶

    A SensitivitySource (one port) may give entries that are not finite, as a difference of finite runs that overflows, so Curvature::gauss_newton finds the first (Sensitivities::first_not_finite) before it reads any term, and refuses it with a MethodError::Failed that names the observation, its part and, for a derivative, the parameter (check_finite). The curvature does not show an entry that it does not read: a derivative in a term of no curvature (a weight of 0, a noise whose information underflows) and the prediction of a loss, whose curvature is finite with any weight. The gradient of the term is then not finite, in the meat of the sandwich and in information matching. The rule does not read the weights, as a run that is not finite is refused whatever the weights (ForwardModel::run_into), and the predictions and derivatives come from the same runs. It therefore also refuses a Laplace approximation whose matrix is right without the entry: one of the Hessian, or one with the entry in a term of weight 0. A loss with no curvature is refused before the derivatives (Discrepancy::check_curvature), since no other units give it one. A curvature that is not finite in its units is refused after it is summed: with finite derivatives only an infinite information gives one, a mean on the edge of the means of its model.

    Alternative Why not
    Check in FiniteDifferences Another source of SensitivitySource, a public trait, could bring them back to the curvature.
    Check that the gradients are finite They do not say which part is not finite, and a Laplace approximation reads none of them. With a weight of 0, the gradient of an observation model is 0 at any prediction, so it does not show one that is not finite.
    Skip the terms of weight 0 An observation would be refused or not by its weight, when a run that is not finite never is.
    Skip what the method at hand does not read The curvature is one object that each method reads in part, so each would need its own rule.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_jacobian_that_is_not_finite_is_refused, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_prediction_that_is_not_finite_is_refused, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_difference_of_finite_runs_that_overflows_is_refused, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_mean_on_the_edge_of_its_model_has_no_finite_curvature, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_loss_with_no_curvature_is_refused_before_a_derivative_that_is_not_finite, crates/commol-inference/src/sensitivity/mod.rs::the_first_entry_that_is_not_finite_names_its_place, tests/uncertainty/test_local.py::TestSandwich::test_sandwich_refuses_derivatives_that_are_not_finite.

    The curvature is kept in units of its parameters¶

    The units give H_u = S Ĥ S: Ĥ, the gradients of the terms and the curvature of a block (the scaled accessors) are in a unit of each parameter, and S = diag(s) (Curvature::scales) is dθ/du times a power of two, so a change of unit rounds nothing. For Gauss–Newton the power is the one at the largest √c |A| of the parameter over the arguments of the terms (units), kept as its exponent, the sum of those of √c and |A|: the product of a finite root and a finite derivative can be above the largest number. For the Hessian it is 1/q_j, with q_j the power of two of the step h_j: the differences are those of v ↦ f(v · q) at fit / q with steps h / q, the same runs, and s_j is the quotient (dθ/du)_j / q_j, since the product with 1/q_j overflows for a small step. The gradients of the terms of the Hessian are those of Gauss–Newton times s_GN / s_H. No method forms H_u: the Laplace approximation and the mixture read S⁻¹ Ĥ⁻¹ S⁻¹, the sandwich the influences S⁻¹ Ĥ⁻¹ ŝ_b, and information matching only traces, which are the same in any units. With bounds that move with a unit that is a power of two, the Laplace approximation and the sandwich of either curvature, η and ν are the same, bit for bit, at the units the guards read, from 2⁻¹⁰⁰⁰ to 2¹⁰⁰⁰. A covariance that leaves the units can still be too large or too small for a number (below).

    Alternative Why not
    H_u as summed, scaled to a diagonal of 1 before its inverse What the sum has lost does not come back: a curvature below the smallest normal number has fewer digits the smaller it is, one below any number is 0, and the squares of the scores that a sandwich sums leave the range first.
    The unit √H_jj in place of a power of two Known only after the sum, and a division by it rounds.
    For the Hessian, the power of two at the root of its diagonal Known only after the differences, whose points already read the step; the unit of the step is known before them.
    The logarithm or the hypotenuse of each sum A second arithmetic for a range that a change of units gives.
    The unit as a number, the power of two of the product The product overflows when the root of the information times a finite derivative is above the largest number, and the unit falls back to 1 with a curvature that is not finite.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_smc.rs::information_matching_does_not_depend_on_the_unit_of_a_parameter, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_covariance_does_not_depend_on_the_unit_of_a_parameter, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_unit_holds_a_derivative_whose_product_with_the_root_of_its_information_overflows, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_scale_is_a_power_of_two_times_the_derivative_of_its_parameter, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_sandwich_reads_scores_whose_squares_underflow, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_local.rs::a_term_of_no_weight_adds_nothing_whatever_its_derivative, crates/commol-inference/src/transform.rs::the_derivative_does_not_depend_on_the_unit_of_the_bounds, tests/uncertainty/test_information_matching.py::TestInformationMatching::test_information_matching_does_not_depend_on_the_scale_of_a_parameter, tests/uncertainty/test_singular_curvature.py::test_a_sandwich_does_not_depend_on_the_scale_of_a_parameter, tests/uncertainty/test_local.py::TestSandwich::test_a_sandwich_reads_scores_whose_squares_overflow.

    One rule says what the data do not determine¶

    Curvature::inverse (InverseCurvature, local/inverse.rs) inverts the curvature for the Laplace approximation, the sandwich, the mixture and information matching, on symmetric_inverse of commol_statistics: the matrix is scaled to a diagonal of 1, and it is singular when its smallest eigenvalue is at most p·ε times the largest (singular, which collinearity_index also reads), the size of the rounding of an eigenvalue of 0. A singular curvature is a MethodError::Invalid with one message, and the mixture words it in the note of the end that gives no component. BlockShares::new inverts the Gauss–Newton curvature of the blocks by it, with its own message. The rule reads neither the scale of a parameter nor the estimated error of the derivatives.

    Two parameters that the model reads only through their product have an eigenvalue of 0 but for the rounding of the sums, which grows with the number of observations. With exact derivatives of a·b·(1 + t), a Poisson model and a, b uniform in (0.1, 2), a plain sum leaves it above p·ε for 5 of 1000 pairs at 200 observations and for 38 at 5000, and the compensated sums of add_term (CompensatedSum, commol_statistics::numeric) for none. A diagonal entry that is at most ε times the sum of the sizes of its terms (CompensatedSum::magnitude) is the rounding of terms that cancel, and symmetric sets it to 0: the parts of a window can read a parameter that the total of the window does not, as in l·(t + a(1 − cos 2πt)/2π), and the rounding of their sum would otherwise pass the rule. The error of finite differences is not a rounding: with a long series it can hide a product, which the rule does not read (the guide says so). The inverse is L⁻ᵀ L⁻¹ of the Cholesky factor of the scaled matrix, and none when an entry of it is not finite. It keeps the digits of a coupling far below the others, which the sandwich of a parameter that the data read weakly needs.

    With a curvature that the data determine, a covariance that leaves the units can still be too large or too small for a number. An entry above the largest number is refused by Gaussian::new (MethodError::Invalid). A variance of 0 is one below the smallest number, since Ĥ⁻¹_jj is above 0 once the rule passes: InverseCurvature::covariance, which also takes the curvature scale of the target of a mixture, refuses it, and InverseCurvature::sandwich refuses it when a scaled influence on the parameter is not 0. The error is a MethodError::Failed that names the parameter and says that its curvature is too large for the covariance to be a number. A sandwich whose block scores are all 0 gives a covariance of 0, which Gaussian::new refuses. A subnormal variance is given, and information matching reads no covariance. The check is in InverseCurvature, where every covariance leaves the units.

    Alternative Why not
    The sign of a pivot (a Cholesky factor, an LU inverse that is finite) The pivot of a direction that the data do not read is a rounding of either sign: the Laplace approximation gives a sd of 1e5, and information matching blames the blocks.
    A rule that reads the estimated error of the derivatives It is an estimate, and a coarse solver would turn a curvature that the data determine into a singular one; the note says how uncertain an entry is.
    A larger limit, or one that grows with the observations It refuses a direction that the data determine, the more so the more data there are.
    A diagonal entry kept as summed The rounding of parts that cancel is of either sign and size: the Laplace approximation of a window in 3, 4 or 12 parts accepts 77 to 93 of 200 random fits, with sd of a on the unbounded scale from 9e7 to 8e9.
    The singular values of √W A A root of the information of each term, and the Hessian has none.
    The inverse from the eigenvectors, which the rule has at hand Their entries carry an absolute rounding of ε, far above a coupling of 1e-30 (the_inverse_keeps_the_digits_of_a_weak_coupling).
    A variance of 0 refused in each method, or any 0 on the diagonal of a sandwich Three checks for one condition; a sandwich whose block scores are all 0 would be blamed on its curvature.
    A message that the data do not determine the parameter They do: the curvature is too large for the range of a number, as a unit too small for it.
