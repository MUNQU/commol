    Scores and distribution functions¶

    Back to Design decisions

    Where the scores, the Fisher information and the distribution functions of the observation models live, and what they give at the ends of a support. The methods that read them are on Local approximations.

    Scores and information live in commol-calibration¶

    Discrepancy::term_derivatives (crates/commol-calibration/src/discrepancy.rs) gives each observation's gradient and expected curvature over its arguments: −2 w n² r and 2 w n² for the sum of squares, the same times c = 1 / (2 √(W · S)) for the root mean squared error (its derivative per unit of S), −w times Noise::scores and w times Noise::information for an observation model. A term of several parts reads the density of its reading (sum_scores and sum_information of likelihood/sums.rs): its scores over the mean of each part, which the curvature chains with the block of the parts, and its expected information; for "exact" both come from the recursion and from its derivative in φ, the information summed over the counts from 0 until the terms above the mean are negligible, with the recursion tilted towards M plus 10 standard deviations. The information of the moments under "exact" is off by up to −15 %/+27 % and gives η of 0.90–0.96 for a correct model. The mean absolute error is refused: its sandwich needs a density of the residuals at 0 and a bandwidth. The sandwich, information matching and later gradient methods read them.

    A count at an end of the means of its model has the limit of its score, the one-sided derivative of its log density (0 there): −1 for a count of 0 at a mean of 0 (Poisson, negative binomial with a φ score of 0, binomial), 1 for a binomial count of n at a mean of n; a sum of parts of mean 0 reads the same through its total or the exact recursion. Its information stays infinite for the mean. When the model holds the mean there (a series that stays 0 for a while), its Jacobian is 0, so the term adds nothing to the gradients, the curvature (add_term) or the floors of a profile (a part with no prediction error is skipped). The sandwich and information matching then give the same result with and without it for a given block_length at positions other series share; it still counts in the blocks and in the residuals of "auto" (#517). Likelihood::term_derivatives refuses a score that is not finite, as it refuses a mean outside its model, naming the observation (MethodError::Invalid): no probability at the fit, outside the support, or an overflow next to the edge. No reader of the terms sums a NaN. Information matching reads an inverse of the curvature that is not finite as singular.

    Alternative Why not
    Derivatives in each method Every method would repeat the models and their noise arguments.
    A kernel density for the absolute error It hides a bandwidth choice; the bootstrap needs none.
    Drop the observations at the end of their means before the scores The density and the information already read them; a second rule would let the readers disagree.
    Skip a Jacobian row of 0 in the gradient of a term The score stays NaN, apart from its density, and an infinite score of a value with no probability would pass unreported.
    Refuse a score that is not finite in each reader of the gradients One rule in several methods, and the floors of a profile read the derivatives without a curvature.

    Distribution functions live in commol-calibration¶

    Noise::tails (likelihood/density.rs) gives P(Y < y), p(y) and P(Y > y) of every model, Noise::sum_tails (likelihood/sums.rs) those of a sum of parts by its reading (the cumulative sums of the recursion for "exact"), and Likelihood::tails those of each observation, at the nearest mean its model allows. Both tails come back, each accurate in its own tail: Φ(8.3) is 1 in f64, so a quantile residual read from F alone would be infinite in the upper tail. The special functions live whole in crates/commol-statistics/src/special.rs, one copy for the distributions of the priors and for the models: the factor of the incomplete gamma and beta reads Stirling's series from a shape of 10, the incomplete beta takes x and 1 − x, and p(y) of a count model is that factor, so a tail of a count of 10⁴ keeps 10 digits. The series and continued fraction of the incomplete gamma take up to 16 √a terms, so a Poisson of mean 10⁸ keeps them too. A negative binomial of φ ≤ 1e-8 has the tails of the Poisson. Two limits remain, from the conditioning of the continued fraction of the beta at a large shape: a negative binomial of size 1/φ keeps about 10⁻¹⁷/φ, and a Student t about 10⁻¹⁶ ν, as its log density. An "exact" sum reads P(Y > y) as a complement down to 0.01, and sums the counts above y beyond that, O(n²) in the last count. The predictive mixes the tails of its draws (ObservationBounds, crates/commol-inference/src/scores.rs), the one primitive of the PIT, the quantile residuals and every later reader.

    Alternative Why not
    A distribution function next to draw_observation in commol-inference The calibration crate could not read it for the sums of parts.
    F alone Its upper tail rounds to 1, and a residual there is infinite.
    The PIT from the draws of the noise It carries the Monte Carlo error of the draws, and a sample of one point gives 0 or 1.
