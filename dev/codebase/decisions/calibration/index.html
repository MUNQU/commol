    Calibration¶

    Back to Design decisions

    What weights, bounds, the observation operator and constraints mean in a calibration.

    One meaning of an observation weight¶

    LossConfig::term and LossConfig::finish in crates/commol-calibration/src/types.rs compute every loss: sse is Σ w·r², rmse is √(Σ w·r² / Σ w), mae is Σ w·|r| / Σ w. The likelihood of observation models (Likelihood::pointwise in crates/commol-calibration/src/likelihood/mod.rs) is Σ w·log p: a weight is a power of its term, so a weight of 2 counts the observation twice in a likelihood as in every loss. The discrepancy (discrepancy.rs) calls them for the optimizer and for every method that reads the loss.

    Rule: never write a loss formula or a log density of an observation elsewhere.

    Guards: commol-calibration/src/types.rs loss_tests; discrepancy::tests; crates/commol-calibration/tests/test_likelihood.rs::a_weight_of_two_is_the_observation_twice.

    A problem has a loss or observation models¶

    A Discrepancy measures predictions by a loss or by the negative log-likelihood of one observation model per series label (DiscrepancyConfig), never both: the fit and the posterior would answer different questions about one problem. Neither has a default, because what to fit is a modelling choice. A model takes the prediction of the observation operator as its mean and only adds the scatter; its noise arguments are numbers or parameters of type Noise, which the value writer skips, as it skips factors. What only makes sense for a loss refuses observation models: normalize_observations, the replicate summaries of a stochastic engine (the likelihood of a summary ignores the noise of the process), LossDistance of ABC and information matching of η.

    Alternative Why not
    Both, the loss for the optimizer and the likelihood for samplers The fit and the posterior would answer different questions.
    Keep "sse" by default and refuse only a loss written next to models The rule would depend on whether a field was written, and the library would choose what to fit.
    A noise parameter of type parameter The value writer would send it to the engine, and reports would mix it with model parameters.
    Densities from a dependency Their values change with its version; SciPy reference values in a test check ours.

    Guards: crates/commol-calibration/tests/test_likelihood.rs, tests/uncertainty/test_observation_models.py.

    Nelder-Mead searches inside the bounds¶

    The objective clamps values to their bounds, so the loss is flat outside them, and a simplex that crosses a bound collapses there. Nelder-Mead therefore searches an unbounded u and scores x = lo + (hi − lo)·(sin u + 1)/2 (SineTransform and BoundedProblem of the argmin fork, in argmin::core::bounds; SearchSpace::transform in optimization.rs builds the transform and refuses an infinite bound). Its evaluations and its result are recorded in x. converged is true only for SolverConverged and TargetCostReached (optimization::converged). An error of the model at a vertex of the initial simplex stops the run with its message (NelderMead::init of the argmin fork returns it).

    Rule: report and record values in x, never in u. Every optimizer run, the verbose one included, goes through optimization::run.

    Guards: optimization::tests; tests/calibration/test_optimizers.py.

    A Simulation follows its model¶

    Simulation keeps the JSON its engine was built from. run, engine and simulation_outputs compare it with model_dump_json() and rebuild the engine when it differs (Simulation._sync_engine), and run resets the engine first. Calibrator takes simulation.engine when it is created, so the check is in the property, not only in run.

    Guard: tests/simulation/test_runs.py.

    One observation operator¶

    ObservationOperator (crates/commol-calibration/src/observation.rs) is the only code that turns a run into observed quantities: the row of each observation, the sum of its outputs, its increment over its window and its factor. The loss, the predictions of PyCalibrationProblem, the predictive's at_observations and every score read it, and the figures read a windowed or scaled series of a predictive through it. The calibration problem is split into one layer per job, each in its own module: ForwardModel (forward.rs) is the only code that clones, resets, writes values to or runs the engine; Feasibility (feasibility.rs) checks bounds and constraints; Discrepancy (discrepancy.rs) compares predictions with data; OptimizationObjective (objective.rs) is the argmin cost function. CalibrationProblem holds them, is built once and has no interior mutability; the buffers of an evaluation are a Workspace per thread.

    Rule: Python never transforms a prediction of a calibration problem or of a sample: their windows, sums, factors, normalization, quantiles and scores come from Rust. Calibrator builds the Rust problem once (commol_rs.inference.CalibrationProblem) and every call reads it. The helpers that present results already computed, such as windowed_totals (api/windows.py) for a run or its replicates, are not predictions; they must agree with the operator, which tests/simulation/test_windows.py::TestAgreementWithTheCalibrationLoss checks.

    An observation is given by at and window, so its window starts at at − window. A quantity given by its start, Gain(start, end), passes the start itself (ObservationOperator::with_starts): end − (end − start) can round away from start, as 0.4 − (0.4 − 0.1) is 0.09999999999999998. The operator finds the row of each position and of each start with the tolerance of the axis (observation::index_at: equal within 1e-9 of the larger of their magnitudes and 1, as index_at of api/results.py), so a start computed as at − window reads the row of the position it rounds away from.

    Guards: observation::tests, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_predictive.rs::observation_draws_are_the_predictions_the_loss_compares, tests/uncertainty/test_predictive.py::TestObservations, crates/commol-inference/tests/test_global_sensitivity.rs::a_gain_reads_its_start_exactly, tests/uncertainty/test_sensitivity.py::TestPropagation::test_a_gain_reads_its_start_exactly.

    Constraints bound the support¶

    A point is feasible, or it violates the problem by an amount: a bound, a constraint, an infeasible start or run, a value that is not finite, or a predicted mean outside the means its observation model allows (Violation in feasibility.rs). Only OptimizationObjective turns a violation into a penalty, base × (1 + amount), where base (Discrepancy::penalty_base) is above the discrepancy of a poor fit: 1000 · n · max_obs² for a loss, and 1000 · Σ wᵢ tᵢ for observation models, with tᵢ the cost of observation i at the narrowest noise the bounds allow and a mean max_obs from it; at least 1e10. An inference method sees the same violation as the edge of the support: its log density is −∞ (discrepancy_in_support in crates/commol-inference/src/target.rs).

    Alternative Why not
    The penalty for every method Its scale is arbitrary, so a sampler would give weight to infeasible points.
    Reject and draw again, with no value A profile or a Laplace fit needs the value at the edge.
    The base of a loss for a likelihood A normal model of a small sd costs more than it, so infeasible points would look better.
    The worst feasible cost seen so far State in the objective: the result would depend on the order of evaluations and on threads.

    Guards: feasibility::tests, crates/commol-calibration/tests/test_infeasible_runs.rs, crates/commol-calibration/tests/test_likelihood.rs::the_penalty_is_above_every_feasible_cost.

    A subproblem holds one quantity fixed¶

    Subproblem (crates/commol-calibration/src/subproblem.rs) holds one parameter or one prediction while optimize_subproblem (optimization.rs) fits the others with the problem's own optimizer. A held parameter leaves the search space (SearchSpace): the optimizer searches one dimension less, and the cost puts the fixed value back before it scores a point. A held prediction g(θ) = c is an equality constraint met by the augmented Lagrangian of the argmin fork, whose factory builds the problem's optimizer at each outer iteration: Nelder-Mead on the sine transform, from the point of the iteration before, or a swarm seeded by replicate_seed(seed, k). The prediction is read through the observation operator of a copy of the problem whose runs also report at its positions (CalibrationProblem::observing), so one run gives the objective and the constraint, and the cost keeps the constraint of the last point it scored. A point that is not feasible keeps its penalty and a constraint of 0. Each point starts from Algencan's scaled penalty; the multiplier is the warm start.

    Alternative Why not
    Bounds [v, v] for a held parameter Nelder-Mead keeps a vertex for a dimension that moves nothing.
    A growing quadratic penalty on g − c It meets the constraint only as μ → ∞, and a large μ makes a narrow valley where Nelder-Mead stops early.
    A second run for the prediction Twice the model runs of each evaluation.
    μ = 100·|Δλ/Δc| from the two points before In the experiment of #132 (the ABC model of tests/support/ with a normal model, the problem of the_multiplier_of_a_prediction_profile_is_minus_its_slope), B at 30 took 5465 evaluations against 3179 with Algencan's μ₀, C at 24 5405 against 3175, for the same intervals and a mean error of the multipliers of 0.8e-4 against 1.2e-4; 10⁴·|Δλ/Δc| gained 4 %. The stop rule reads ‖g − c‖, which a small μ leaves large.

    A swarm without stop rules runs each outer iteration to its max_iterations, so a held prediction stops at the outer limit and never reports convergence.

    Guards: crates/commol-inference/tests/test_profiles.rs (fixing_a_parameter_at_the_fit_keeps_the_loss, fixing_a_prediction_at_the_fit_keeps_the_parameters, a_problem_observing_more_positions_scores_as_the_problem, a_swarm_fits_a_subproblem_and_repeats_from_its_seed).

    A result records its seed¶

    Every random number of a calibration and of its uncertainty methods comes from Calibrator.seed: problem.seed, or a seed the calibrator draws once and logs. A particle swarm takes it too, so the seed alone repeats a run. The result records it, so a result read from a file repeats too: CalibrationResult.seed, and settings["seed"] of the SampleMethod of every sample a runner makes (sample_method in api/inference/resampling.py) or Calibrator.point_sample makes.

    Guards: tests/calibration/test_problem.py (test_a_result_records_the_seed_of_its_calibration); tests/calibration/test_optimizers.py (test_the_seed_of_a_result_repeats_a_particle_swarm).
